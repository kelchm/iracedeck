# Replay Control dial: Lap, Session, Incident and Car stepping

> **Issue:** [#801](https://github.com/niklam/iracedeck/issues/801) · **Supersedes:** _none_ · **Superseded by:** _none_
>
> Point-in-time design record. The code and `.claude/rules/` are the truth; this is not documentation.

Four more modes on the dial that #1316 and #1317 build (`2026-10-04-issue-1316-replay-control-dial.md`). The lap and session steps are #1426's (`2026-10-12-issue-1426-replay-record-steps.md`); this spec says what a turn of the dial does with them, and nothing about how the record is made. It is built last of the replay work (order agreed 2026-10-12: #1276, the dial, #1354, #1430, #1426, this issue), on #1430's shared gate for replay commands sent while the replay is moving. #1437, which shows on the dial's box that a turn is still being worked on, is built after it (order of 2026-10-13: #1354, #1430, #1426, this issue, #1437).

## The decision

The dial's Mode select gains **Lap**, **Session**, **Incident** and **Track Order** (named Car until the maintainer's second test round, 2026-10-18: it is Camera Controls' Track Order rotation, so it takes that name and its stored value `track-order`; nothing had shipped under `car`). Everything the surface owns is unchanged and shared: the press and gesture slots, the remembered-speed Play / Pause, the in-replay guard, the throttle and the dash box.

| Mode | A detent | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lap | the viewed car's next / previous lap | #1426's record step, else iRacing's search |
| Session | the next / previous session | #1426's record step, else iRacing's search |
| Incident | the next / previous incident | iRacing's search |
| Track Order | the next / previous car on track | iRacing's Next Car / Previous Car bindings |

Clockwise is next in all four. Lap, Session and Incident are ordered by time in the recording and Car is iRacing's own Next Car, the car ahead, so none of them is a number-primary rotation and none flips (`encoders-and-touchscreen.md` rule 4).

## Lap and Session: one jump per turn

A turn of `ticks` detents calls #1426's walk with `steps = |ticks|` and makes **one** waited jump to the entry it reaches, through the shared jump sequence (#1276). So a fast spin of five detents is one jump five laps on, and there is no per-event cap: the list's end is the clamp (rule 3).

- **A turn during a jump steps from where that jump is going.** The anchor is the plugin's one record of where the replay is headed (#1430, built from Frame Jog's rule in `replay-position-state.ts`; amended 2026-10-12), the value Frame Jog measures its own turns from. The new jump takes the cursor from the old one and inherits the speed it captured, which #1276 already handles. This is the shape the sim was measured to take (#1426's sim facts, 2026-10-12): an absolute jump sent during a seek retargets it and the last target wins, so a fast spin needs no queue. The target is never computed from telemetry read mid-seek, which shows frame 0, and the record's newest target stands until its frame has read back in a way that can only mean the move is done (#1426). On a playing replay the first jump pauses it and waits for the pause to show before it seeks. A turn that arrives in that wait is a jump into a jump under way: it moves the target and its seek goes out at once, with no second pause, and nothing more is sent when the pause reads back (#1430, amended 2026-10-13, after a pause and then a jump at once on a playing replay landed on the jump's frame and rested paused 57 of 57).
- **The record ran out part-way:** the jump goes as far as the record reaches and the rest of the turn is dropped. The next detent finds nothing that way and goes to the search below.
- **The record has nothing that way** (zero steps): the whole turn goes to the paced search.
- **A turn made while a key's search has not landed jumps at once**, and the search is forgotten (added 2026-10-13). A record jump sends a position jump, which wins over a search on its way: 8 of 8 on a replay playing at 1x, 50 and 100 ms into a 22375-frame session search, with the pause read back and the jump on its frame (#1430). Only the search runner, which sends no position jump, waits for a search.
- **While the search runner is working for this dial's target**, a turn goes to its count without asking the record: the cursor is between searches, and there is no landing to step from (added 2026-10-12).

## The paced search

iRacing's lap, session and incident searches cannot be chained: one sent before the last has landed leaves the cursor part-way (the fastest-lap walk waits for exactly this reason). A dial sends detents far faster than a search lands, so the dial paces its own sends.

**Measured, and the queue stays** (2026-10-12, #1426's sim facts). On a paused replay, three Next Lap 200 ms apart or closer ended in the wrong place: the first landed at once and the other two became one more move about 430 ms later. But one search sent 100 ms after the last had read back landed exactly one step on, 16 of 16 (paused, playing, and after a long jump), carried out by the sim about 400 ms after the one before it landed. So the rule is **one search on its way at a time**, and that is a read-back and not a clock. Two more things were measured that the runner is built around. A search sent in the very poll that first reads a landing can be lost: 5 of 9, and 3 of 3 after a long jump. And a search sent during a position jump ended the jump where it stood, as a pause did, so no replay command but a position jump is ever sent into a seek. A search is itself a seek, one that telemetry does not show (2026-10-13, #1430's captures): it runs at iRacing's 2048 frames a sim update, so 475 frames landed inside 20 ms and 22375 took about 195 ms, and a pause, a speed or a frame step sent into one on its way cancelled it, 24 of 24, and was not acted on itself once the search's first sim update had passed. So nothing follows a search bare until it has landed. What may follow one that has not is an absolute position jump, which wins over a search on its way and is a move of one frame without one: a jump to the frame beside the reading put the replay right 14 of 14, into a far search and after one with nothing to find, and a search sent once that jump's landing had held landed 5 of 5 (#1430's takeover, measured 2026-10-13).

One runner per process, in `replay-control/replay-search-queue.ts`, because iRacing has one cursor. **It is a policy on #1430's gate, not a second gate** (agreed with #1430's author, 2026-10-12). The gate owns the two waits the steps below use, written once for every replay key and dial: waiting until the replay has settled, with a read-back held for a number of reads, and waiting for a search to land. Since 2026-10-13 it owns a third step as well, settling the replay by a jump when a search has not landed (step 4). The runner keeps what is the dial's own: the count, its clamp, its patience, the second try and the playback step. Each search it sends is registered with the gate as under way, so a key's search, speed or frame step waits behind it (a pause is not sent at all, step 5). A key pressed during a run takes the cursor, which ends the run, and what that press then waits for is the gate's to say (behind a search it waits for the landing, and pressed again it acts at once, #1430).

**The runner's patience** (amended 2026-10-13, a second time). For a day the landing wait of each search was the figure the gate works out for how long a search could still be running: the time the frames between the cursor and the edge of the tape it runs towards take at 2048 frames a sim update and 60 updates a second, doubled, and never under the fastest-lap walk's wait (four times the Fastest Lap Search Delay, 1.6 s at its default). The gate no longer waits by that figure, and the runner cannot either. The gate is now built for recordings of four to six hours (maintainer, 2026-10-13), where the figure is about 21 s from the start of the tape, and a turn past the last incident would hold the replay for twice that. So the runner waits for a landing with a patience of its own, as #1430 asks of it: **that figure, never under 1.6 s and capped at three seconds** (`SEARCH_PATIENCE_MIN_MS` and `SEARCH_PATIENCE_MAX_MS`, one constant each; the walk's wait takes the place of either where the Fastest Lap Search Delay setting makes it the longer). The floor is the runner's own and the setting can raise it and not lower it (coordinator, 2026-10-13): a search that follows another was carried out about 400 ms after the first landed (#1426's sim facts), and the gate's figure has only the walk's wait under it, which is 200 ms at the setting's minimum. The cap is what bounds the cost of step 4. Nothing correct rests on either: when the patience runs out with a search still on its way, the settle jump of step 4 ends that search before anything else is sent. **What the cap gives up is a search further away than three seconds of seeking**, about 370000 frames or an hour and forty minutes of recording at the measured rate, and less on a slower machine: a session step across a longer session in a recording made without the plugin, or an incident further off than that. The dial ends such a turn as it ends one with nothing to find. The keypad's key still reaches it, since a key's search is waited for however long, and with the plugin's record a Session turn is one jump of any length. The cap is this spec's choice, agreed by the coordinator, and goes to the maintainer as such; it is one constant if he wants it higher. Neither the cap nor the cost of step 4 applies to the first search of a run on a replay playing at 1x or slower (below).

1. **A turn adds its signed detents to a pending count** for its target (lap, session or incident). A turn the other way subtracts, and can carry the count through zero. A turn on a dial set to another target replaces the count.
2. **The runner is a replay jump** (`beginReplayJump`), and it holds the cursor. In this order: whatever of the plugin's is still on its way when it takes the cursor is waited for first; then it pauses the replay if it still reads moving, and waits for the pause to read back. What can be on its way is a record jump, whose frame it waits to read back (a jump that had not sent its seek yet still sends it, since only a position jump replaces a seek, #1430), or a key's search that may be on its way. That search it waits to land, for its patience, and if it has not landed it settles the replay by the gate's jump (step 4), which pauses a moving replay itself (amended 2026-10-13, twice: a pause sent into a search on its way cancels the search and is not acted on itself, and since the gate keeps its record of a search until a landing or a jump, no wait running out makes a bare pause safe). The speed the runner captures for its playback step is the one `beginReplayJump` hands it, which behind a search is the speed the replay had when the search went out and not a reading taken behind it: a search clears slow motion (#1430, 4 of 4). With nothing on its way, nothing is awaited between the runner taking the cursor and that pause going out, so a Pause press can never find the cursor of a quiet replay held by a runner that has not paused it yet (#1430's rule for a pause rests on that; the one runner that holds the cursor of a replay it has not paused is the one whose first search went out before the pause, below, and the gate knows it from its own record of the pauses it has sent, so the runner tells it nothing); while the runner waits behind a jump or a search, a Pause press is the gate's to place, as it is behind either one without the runner. The pause never goes into a seek (a pause 170 ms into a jump left the replay 8700 frames off, 3 of 3), and a jump of #1276's has already paused the replay it is crossing. While the count is not zero it sends one search toward zero, waits for it to land (the gate's landing wait, for the runner's patience), and takes one off the count. Every one of these waits is a read-back, and no step goes out on a timer.
3. **A read-back has to hold before a search follows it.** The reading a search goes out on, whether the pause, an inherited landing or the last search's landing, must show on three reads 50 ms apart. That is the read-back form of the 100 ms that worked 16 of 16, where a search sent on the first read was lost more often than not; nothing between the two was measured, and 100 ms on a fast machine is a lower bound.
4. **A search that has not landed within the patience is settled by a jump and sent once more** (amended 2026-10-13, twice). A lost search, a search with nothing left to find and a search still on its way look the same on a paused replay, and the hold in step 3 may be too short on a slower machine; the second try puts a lost step back. Before it goes out the runner settles the replay by the gate's own step (#1430: an absolute jump to the frame beside the reading, one frame forward, or one back inside the end-of-tape margin, judged as moved and then held). That jump ends a search that was still running, and after it a search is safe whatever the first was doing; the second try sent bare, as this spec had it, could be a search sent into a search. A second search that does not land ends the run, and the runner settles once more as it ends, for two reasons: a search still on its way must not move the replay after the dial has given up, and the gate's record of it is then cleared, so the next key press acts at once where behind a record left standing it would wait for as long as the search could run. A settle jump that moves nothing within its own wait ends the run with nothing more sent. The cost is at the end of what there is to find: two patiences and two one-frame jumps, between about three and a half and seven seconds on the maintainer's machine wherever in the recording it happens, with the replay left paused a frame or two from where it stood. Under the first amendment of the day it was up to 42 s at the start of a six-hour recording.
5. **It ends** when the count reaches zero and is still zero once the landing has held (a turn made during that hold carries the run on; amended 2026-10-17, from the review), when a search has not landed twice running (the end of what there is to find, which on a paused replay the sim marks in no other way, or a search beyond the patience: the rest of the count is dropped and the replay is settled, step 4), when a search, the pause or an inherited landing never reads back (a warning, the rest dropped), or when any other replay command takes the cursor. Ending at zero applies the **Replay jump playback** setting through `applyReplayJumpPlayback`, on a landing that has held as in step 3; every other ending sends no playback step, and nothing at all but the settle jump of step 4. What the command that took the cursor does next is the gate's to say (#1430). One case is asked of it here: a pause pressed during a run sends nothing, because the runner has paused the replay already, just as a pause during a jump sends nothing. It takes the cursor, which ends the run with the replay paused. The exception is the first search of a run that went out before the pause (below): the replay is still playing, so a pause pressed then is placed by the gate as it is behind a key's search on a playing replay, held for the landing and then sent.
6. **The count is clamped to ±`SEARCH_QUEUE_MAX` (10)** (maintainer, 2026-10-12), measured on what is left after the search on its way, so the caption never reads more than ten (amended 2026-10-17, from the review). The sim carries out a search that follows another about 400 ms after the first landed, so with the hold a step is about half a second on the maintainer's machine and more on a slower one or in a long replay. Ten is then five seconds or more of the replay moving after the hand has left the dial; a longer spin is more likely a slip than a request. The record path has no such limit because it is one jump whatever the count.
7. **A run counts the replay as on screen while a move of the plugin's own is under way** (added 2026-10-17, from the review): a target still standing or a seek showing, as the dial surface counts it. In a live session the debounced replay state gives a long seek up after its one-second grace, and a run behind a long record jump would otherwise end at once as the replay left, with that jump's playback step lost with it.
8. **Behind a jump the run takes the gate's verdict where it can read it.** A plain position jump is judged by its frame. A target the gate's takeover or settle jump made can land a few frames off and still be a landing by the gate's rule; the merged gate does not export how a target ended, so the run reads a target that left the record before its own deadline as landed and one that outlived it as given up (as built, 2026-10-17). That misreads two narrow cases, both named at the check: a landing in the last poll before the deadline reads as given up, and a takeover recorded with no replay reading on screen reads as landed when it moved nothing. Exporting the target's fate from the gate would make it exact; that is proposed as a follow-up.
9. **What the runner sends reaches the speed every surface shows**: its pause and its playback step are mirrored to the dials and keys as a record jump's are, for the surface's whole life and not only while a dial is on screen, and the run's own cursor take keeps a dial's pending speed as the dial's record jump does (added 2026-10-17, from the review and the second read).
10. **A replay jump started while the runner's search is on its way keeps the user's speed** (added 2026-10-17, from the second read). The runner pauses before it searches, so the gate records that search as sent at PAUSED, and a key's record step, a marker jump or a walk started then captured PAUSED and, under Remember state, left the replay paused. `beginReplayJump` now prefers an unapplied pending jump speed (the runner's, or the fastest-lap and race-start walks', which pause first too) over the speed a search went out at. A key's search takes the cursor and so clears the pending speed, so a jump behind it still captures the speed that search went out at, slow motion included (checked with #1430's author).
11. **No jump back to the frame already on screen.** Before a step against the direction of play the runner jumps back to the landing's first frame; when the paused replay already shows that frame it sends none, since a jump to the frame on screen never reads back and would hold the run to the two-minute ceiling (added 2026-10-17, from the second read).

The pause in step 2 is what lets a landing be read without a rule for playback: in a playing replay the frame never holds still, and a frame that playback moved reads like a search that landed unless it is judged against what playback explains, as the gate judges it (#1430). The opening was measured as the runner will send it (a replay playing at 1x, pause, read back, first search): the first search landed 6 of 6. The pause also means the paced search honours the playback setting where the keypad's single search does not; that difference is the keypad's (#1276 leaves its searches as they are) and is not changed here.

**On a replay playing at 1x or slower, the first search of a run goes out before the pause** (coordinator, 2026-10-13). On a playing replay the gate can see what a paused one hides (#1430's captures): a search on its way, by the frame standing still (22 of 22, the session, lap and incident searches at seven speeds from quarter speed to 16x and in reverse), and one that found nothing, by the frame still advancing as playback explains one Fastest Lap Search Delay after the send (14 of 14 at the same speeds). Both were measured for a lone search, which the first search of a run is. So when the runner takes the cursor with nothing of the plugin's on its way, and the replay reads playing no faster than 1x, it sends its first search at once and reads the gate's sign. This is the one change to step 2, and the runner has three outcomes to act on, which the gate's landing wait hands it (#1430, amended the same day for this: the wait takes a patience for a search nothing shows and a hard bound, and resolves landed, found nothing, not landed, gave up or superseded):

- **Found nothing:** the run ends with nothing more sent, about 0.4 s after the turn at the setting's default. The replay was never paused and plays on at the speed it had, slow motion included (the frame kept its own pace in all 14). One detent past the last incident, or Next Session in the last session, costs that and nothing else.
- **On its way:** the runner waits for the landing however long it takes. The sign is telemetry's and not a clock, so the patience does not apply and a first step of any distance lands. The last line is the ceiling #1276 puts on any jump's wait (`REPLAY_JUMP_MAX_WAIT_MS`, two minutes), a give-up that sends nothing and warns.
- **Landed:** one comes off the count. At zero the run ends as step 5 says, by the **Replay jump playback** step on the held landing, and no pause was ever sent: under Remember state a single detent on a replay playing at 1x is the search and nothing else. With the count not at zero the runner pauses on the landing (a pause sent 30 ms or more after a landing was acted on 6 of 6, #1430), waits for it to read back, and carries on from step 3 as on a paused replay, patience and settle jump included.

What surrounds those three:

- **A step against the direction of play needs the cursor put back.** Between the landing and the pause the replay plays on, a few frames at 1x. A further step in the direction of play is unharmed by that, and so is an incident step either way, since that search follows iRacing's own pointer. A lap or session step against it (Previous on a replay playing forwards, Next on one playing in reverse) would start from past the line the first search landed on. So before such a step the runner jumps to the frame the landing first read, once the pause has read back (a pause and then an absolute jump, 57 of 57).
- **What that jump leaves open.** The first reading of a landing can be one frame past it at 1x, and whether iRacing's Previous Lap from one frame past a line goes to the line before or back to the same line is not measured. The capture that settles it is on a paused replay, on any machine: Previous Lap and Previous Session sent from 1, 2 and 6 frames past a line. If it goes back to the same line, a first search against the direction of play takes the paused path, and the rest of this section stands. The same holds if the capture has not been run by the time the runner is written (coordinator, 2026-10-13): the branch is not built on an answer nobody has measured, and a first search against the direction of play then pauses first. Manual step 2 is the proof either way.
- **Faster than 1x takes the paused path**, with its patience and cap. At 16x playback explains sixteen frames a tick, and a landing a few hundred frames on would read as playback and be judged as nothing found.
- **Reverse play at 1x or slower** is treated as forward play is, with the direction of play turned round. The sign was measured in reverse.
- **Slow motion.** A search on its way clears it: a replay at half speed played on at 1x after the landing, and one a step slower at 2x (4 of 4, #1430). The runner's speed was captured before the search, so the playback step at the end puts back the speed the user had. Until the pause or that step, the replay runs at what the search left, for a few tenths of a second.
- **When the sign is wrong.** The 400 ms is a patience in the gate and nothing correct rests on it here either: on a machine that carries a search out later than that, the run ends as found nothing and the search then lands by itself. The step that was turned for has happened, the rest of the count is dropped, and nothing was sent bare. The gate's record of the search stands until a landing or a jump, so a key pressed meanwhile is still placed safely.
- **A runner that starts behind a jump or a key's search** takes the paused path: what it inherits has paused the replay or has to be settled first.

**Not done here: every step of a run on the playing replay.** That would end a spin past the end of a list in under half a second too, and needs no patience at all. It rests on three things nobody has measured: when a search sent straight after another stalls the frame (the 400 ms sign was measured for lone searches, a follow-on search is carried out about 400 ms after the landing before it, and a slower machine cannot be settled from the maintainer's), chained Previous steps on a replay playing forwards, and small leaps at fast-forward. It also forks steps 2, 4 and 5 in two. It is [#1439](https://github.com/niklam/iracedeck/issues/1439). The runner here is written with one function that returns a search's outcome, so that issue adds an outcome and a start branch and does not rewrite it.

## Incident

Always the paced search. #1426 records incidents and compares them with where iRacing's search lands; the mode switches to the record when that issue's follow-up does, for the keys and the dial together.

**The search follows a pointer of iRacing's own, not the cursor** (measured 2026-10-12, #1426's sim facts; the pointer is an inference from the pattern). With the cursor put back on one frame and the camera on one car before every case, a single Next Incident landed 300 frames back, then on the frame, then 300 and 600 frames on, then 300 back again: each search stepped on from the last one's, wherever the cursor had been put. Searches sent one after another always stepped the same way and never back, though the first of a run can land behind the cursor, since a landing is some seconds before its incident. So a detent is one step of iRacing's own, as the key is today, and the pacing adds no error to it. What the dial cannot promise is where the first step lands after the cursor was moved some other way: that is the search's, and the record's to fix in #1426's follow-up. The mode is built on the search as specified.

## Car

One tap of iRacing's Next Car / Previous Car binding per rotate event, through the action's binding dispatch (`CAR_CYCLE_BINDING_KEYS`, V / Shift+V by default, SimHub roles included). One tap per event and not per detent: a keyboard tap holds the key for about 100 ms, and a continued spin arrives as further events. This is what Camera Controls' Track Order dial does, and the two dials share the bindings.

A camera switch in the middle of a fastest-lap walk would point its search at another car, so the tap cancels a walk first, as the keypad's car modes do, and nothing else: it cancels the cursor's owner only when that owner is one of the two walks whose searches ride on the camera's car (Jump to Fastest Lap's and Jump to Race Start's, `FASTEST_LAP_WALK_OWNER` and `RACE_START_WALK_OWNER`), checked by owner, and leaves any other owner standing, so a record jump's run, a marker jump's or the paced search's survives a Car turn. Today's `cancelFastestLapWalk` cannot be reused for it: it is `cancelReplayCursorOwner` under another name and cancels whatever holds the cursor. The check is this issue's to add, as a small helper in `shared/replay-cursor.ts` or in the mode (#1430's author, 2026-10-14); it is `cancelReplayCursorOwnerIf(owners, by)` in `shared/replay-cursor.ts`, which cancels the claim only when its owner is one of `owners` and leaves any other standing (added 2026-10-17, as built; #1430's spec is not amended for it, since #1430 has merged). **A turn made while a position jump is under way waits for the jump** (coordinator, 2026-10-13; the tap went out at once before). A jump is not harmed by the tap, but it swallows it: the Next Car key sent 20, 100 and 200 ms into a jump left the jump on its frame 9 of 9 and the camera on its car 9 of 9, where the same tap on a quiet replay moved it 2 of 2 (#1430's captures). The camera broadcast of 2026-10-12 says the same on a second look at its records: the jump landed 6 of 6, and the three switches that named another car left the camera where it was. A search is different: a camera change sent 50 and 100 ms into a 22375-frame session search was acted on every time it could show, 5 of 5, and the search landed 8 of 8, so a tap does not wait for a search, which would otherwise hold it for as long as a search with nothing to find could still be running. So while a target stands or a seek shows, the mode asks the gate's wait for jumps (#1430: no jump under way, shown on three reads 50 ms apart, the hold a search follows a landing on) and taps when it resolves. That is `waitForReplaySettled` with `jumpsOnly`, passed no claim and bounded by `REPLAY_JUMP_MAX_WAIT_MS`; a jump that starts during it resets its count, and with no claim it ends only settled or at that bound. It is not `waitForReplayJumpsToSettle`, whose bound is fixed when it is called from the target then standing, so a longer jump that starts later would outlast it (#1430's author, 2026-10-14). The wait takes no cursor and cancels nothing, so the jump lands, its playback step runs, and the tap follows. One tap waits at a time for the whole plugin: a further turn meanwhile, on this dial or another, replaces it with its own direction, so however many events arrive during a jump the camera moves by one car. A new jump that starts while a tap waits is still a jump under way, and the tap goes on waiting. **The tap waits for as long as the jump takes** (amended 2026-10-13). It was dropped after three seconds, the limit the gate then put on a waiting key press. The gate has none now: for replay keys the maintainer chose a wait with no cap over one of three seconds, since a press made early in a ten-second jump across a long recording means "when you get there" (#1430), and a turn of this dial means the same. What ends the wait with no tap is a newer turn, which takes its place, the dial leaving the screen, the replay being left, or the ceiling #1276 puts on any jump's wait (`REPLAY_JUMP_MAX_WAIT_MS`, two minutes), which drops it with a debug line. Carrying that choice over from replay keys to the camera tap is this spec's reading and has not been put to him. With no jump under way the tap goes out at once. What a tap does in the first 100 ms after a landing was not measured; the hold is the one proven for a search, and manual step 5 is the proof for the tap. It is the dial's only key-binding mode: the PI shows the binding status line for it, and the box draws the missing-binding warning.

## The display

Amended 2026-10-18 from the maintainer's second test round (the first showed the lap as `L12` over the play state):

| | Lap | Session | Incident | Track Order |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Label | `LAP` | `SESSION` | `INCIDENT` | `TRK ORD` |
| Value | the viewed car's lap at the cursor over the laps in that session, `12/25` | the cursor's session, `RACE` | replay time, `m:ss` | the viewed car, `#12` |
| Caption | the replay time, `m:ss` | the viewed car's `12/25`, as Lap's value | none | none; the car numbers on track behind (left) and ahead (right) stand beside the value, where they can be trusted |
| Side marks | lit where a turn that way has somewhere to go | the same | both lit | both lit |

- **While a paced search runs**, its pending count (`+3`, `-2`) takes the caption in Lap, Session and Incident: the count is progress.
- **The laps in the session** (maintainer, 2026-10-18): the session's scheduled laps (`SessionInfo.Sessions[n].SessionLaps`) when it is a number; for an unlimited session (practice, qualifying, a timed race) the larger of the laps the viewed car completed in it by the session's results (`ResultsPositions[].LapsComplete`) and the highest lap the record holds for that car in that session instance, and the results' count alone once they are official (`ResultsOfficial` 1), since the record's last entry is then the finish crossing; the lap on screen never reads above the count (amended 2026-10-18, from the review: the record numbers a lap as completed laps plus one, so a finisher read `26/25` on the cool-down lap and a live timed session `13/12`). Between a timed race's flag and its results going official the cool-down lap reads `N+1/N+1`; with none of these, no denominator (`12`). Without the record the current lap is unknown, so the value reads `--/25`, or `--`.
- **Track Order's neighbours** (maintainer, 2026-10-18, the third of three options put to him): the car numbers on track ahead and behind, drawn beside the value, smaller and dimmed, as Camera Controls' Car # draws them, in a `neighbours` slot this issue adds to the shared dash box (coordinator, 2026-10-18: #1449 widens the slot later, and every dial that passes none is drawn byte for byte as before, pinned by a baseline of 32 box shapes on both canvases); they shrink to fit beside the value and are left out below 9 px rather than overlap it, shown only where the per-car track positions are the replay's own: in a saved replay, and at the live edge of an in-session replay. Scrubbed back in an in-session replay those arrays read the live field (#1281's capture), so there the car number shows alone. The preview can still differ from where iRacing's binding lands (the pace car is a stop of iRacing's own, #1277); that is accepted. The value keeps the size and place it has in the other modes: a mode with nothing on its caption line reserves the line (the dash box's new `reserveCaptionLine`), since the box draws the value larger without a caption.
- **Long Press** (maintainer, 2026-10-18): a new gesture value `mode-default`, labelled Mode default, is Long Press's default. It runs **Jump to My Car** (Replay Control's keypad mode, now also a gesture option in every slot) in Track Order and Set 1x and Play in every other mode; a specific pick applies in every mode. It extends the stored `REPLAY_DIAL_GESTURES` enum. Long Press is Stream Deck+ only.
- **Every mode keeps its side marks, and a label fits between them** (maintainer, 2026-10-18: "I want the small arrows! If the name is too long, call it TRK ORDER or TRACK ORD"). The marks sit 0.3 of the box width out from the centre and are 10 px wide, which leaves 96 px of label on the Mirabox knob (16 px bold) and 110 px on the Stream Deck+ strip (15 px). `TRACK ORDER` (about 118 px on the knob) ran into them, and `TRK ORDER` and `TRACK ORD` (95 and 96 px) touched them, so the box label is `TRK ORD` (73 px), the maintainer's pick from the two that clear. In an in-session replay the per-car arrays read the live field: four snapshots (2026-09-28 and 2026-09-29) scrubbed back to moments when the camera car was racing all read `CarIdxLap` −1. The record's entry at the cursor is the lap actually on screen, and the maintainer agreed to read it from there (2026-10-12). With no entry for that car at the cursor the lap reads `--` (`--/25`), as the amended table above says (amended 2026-10-18; it was the replay time before the second test round).
- **The session name** is `SessionInfo.Sessions[ReplaySessionNum].SessionType`, upper-cased and fitted by the box.
- **While a turn is being worked through** the caption shows what is left, `+3` or `-2`: the paced search's pending count, or nothing for a record jump, which is one step however far it goes.
- **Side marks for Lap and Session** are dim only when the record has entries and none lies that way. With no record both are lit, since the search may still move.

During a record jump, Lap and Session show the entry at the pending landing: the lap whose start is the last at or before it, and the session whose recorded start is. On any other tick whose raw `IsReplayPlaying` is false inside a replay, the value stays as it last was.
- The views read the store in memory and wait for nothing: `view` runs on every tick.

## What the box shows while a turn is worked on (#1437)

Added 2026-10-13. #1437 (`2026-10-13-issue-1437-replay-key-busy-look.md`) draws three looks in the caption line of the dial's box, read from the state seam of #1430's gate: stepping dots while the dial's own jump or search is under way, three still dots while its command waits behind something else, and a red cross for a second when the plugin gave up. It is built after this issue and touches no send site, and these modes do not exist when #1430 gives every existing send its token. So the one thing it needs of them is built here (coordinator, 2026-10-13): **every replay command a Lap, Session or Incident turn sends carries the dial's origin token**, the opaque string #1430's seam takes on each send, which for a dial is its own context id. The seam's names are #1430's and are not repeated here. This issue draws none of the looks.

- **Where the token is passed.** The record jump's `beginReplayJump`, the runner's `beginReplayJump`, and each search the runner registers with the gate. The surface binds the token into the `io` members those go through, so a mode module never handles it. Speed Control and Frame Jog pass none on a turn, as #1437 has it (a spin is under way for as long as the hand turns), and are not touched.
- **How each turn ends, for the seam.** A replay jump reports its result when it ends (#1430). A record jump reports what #1276's sequence reports. The runner passes `end` landed when the count reaches zero, gave up when a search has not landed twice running, the first search on a playing replay found nothing, a settle jump moved nothing or a read-back never came, and cancelled when another replay command took the cursor, a pause pressed during the run included. The seam reports those as completed, gave up and superseded. A run cut short because the replay was left or the sim went away is filed by the seam as replay-left or disconnected, not as gave up, and draws no cross: only a real give-up of the runner's shows it (amended 2026-10-17, following #1437's spec).
- **A Lap or Session turn answered from the record** shows the dots from the turn until its jump lands, often a fraction of a second, since #1437 has no start delay (maintainer, 2026-10-14). A spin that keeps retargeting is one unbroken run of the dial's token and so one fact, dated from its first turn, and the mark does not blink between detents (#1430's state seam dates a fact from the first command of its token's unbroken run).
- **A paced search** (Incident always, Lap and Session on a miss) shows the dots from the first turn until the run ends. A run that stops at the end of the recording shows the red cross whether it moved some of its steps or none: the rest of the count was dropped. When it is the first search of a run on a playing replay that finds nothing, the cross is all that shows, at about 0.4 s. Spinning past the last incident therefore shows the dots for the two patiences of step 4, some four to seven seconds, and then the cross.
- **None of these turns is ever held in the gate's waiting slot.** A record jump takes over, and the runner holds the cursor while it waits behind a jump or a search, which is under way and not waiting. The still dots show on this dial only for a push or a gesture, whose sends are on `master` today and get their token in #1430.
- **The caption has two claimants.** The paced search's pending count (`+3`) is drawn in the caption line, where #1437 puts its marks, and during a run both hold at once. The count is progress, which a mark cannot show, so the mark is drawn beside it and never replaces it (#1437, amended the same day to say so).
- **Two dials.** A run carries the token of the dial whose turn started it. A second dial on the same target adds to the count and shows it, and shows no mark. A turn that replaces the count for another target starts a run of its own, with its own dial's token, and the first ends superseded.
- **Car passes no token and draws nothing.** It sends no replay command, and #1437 leaves the car and camera keys out with #1435. A tap that waits behind a long jump is the one place a still mark would say something here; that belongs with #1435, where the keypad's car keys get the same wait.
- **Files this touches:** the dial surface, which binds the token; `replay-control-dial-mode.ts`, whose new `io` members carry it; and `replay-search-queue.ts`, whose run takes it and reports its ending. No setting, Property Inspector, manifest or comms change comes of it, and the website's words about the looks are #1437's. If #1437 merges first (its spec allows that should this issue slip), nothing here changes: the token is passed at these sites in this issue either way, and manual step 10 then applies.

## The seam

All additive to what #1316 ships; Speed Control and Frame Jog do not change.

- **`REPLAY_DIAL_MODES`** gains `lap`, `session`, `incident` and `car`, one module each and one row each in `DIAL_MODES`. A stored value an older build does not know reads as Speed Control there (`.catch("speed")`); nothing is migrated.
- **`rotate` may return a promise.** The surface tracks it: it catches a rejection, logs it, and re-renders when it settles. A turn that arrives meanwhile is not dropped, because the modes above take it into the pending landing, the pending count or the one tap that waits.
- **`ReplayDialModeIo` gains** a waited jump built on #1276's jump sequence (`send` stays the cancelling one-shot every other mode relies on), the record step and the search queue, a binding tap, and `isBindingMissing`. The waited jump and the search queue carry the dial's origin token, bound by the surface (added 2026-10-13, for #1437). Frame Jog's fix for a fast spin (#1317) has already put `position()` (where the replay stands or is headed) and `jumpTo()` (an absolute jump that registers its target) on `io`; these modes read the first for their anchor and bring only what is missing.
- **The in-replay guard holds through the dial's own seek**, as #1317 built it (added 2026-10-12, amended the same day once the dial's code could be read). A spin that keeps retargeting keeps the raw flag false until the last jump lands: fifty jumps 60 ms apart read mid-seek for three seconds in the probe. In a live session that outlasts the one-second grace of the debounced replay state (#1324) and would drop detents as turned from the car; in a saved replay the state's frame reads 0 from then on. So the surface counts the replay as on screen while a move of the plugin's own is under way (a cursor claim, or a target that has not read back), for three seconds past the last time the debounced state said so (`REPLAY_TARGET_GIVE_UP_MS`). The bound is deliberate: without it, a move sent on that hold alone would renew the hold for as long as the dial is turned from the car. In a live session a jump that seeks for longer than that therefore drops further turns and dims the box until it lands, which is the safe side. The record jump and the search runner both hold the cursor, so nothing is added here; #1429 is the same gap on the Replay Markers dial.
- **`ReplayDialModeView` gains** an optional `bindingMissing`.
- **The comms catalog's `replay-control-dial` entry** gains the four modes: `api` for three, the car-cycle key pair for `car`.
- **Trigger descriptions** come from each mode's `rotateDescription`.

## What rides on iRacing's seeking

Every mode but Car ends in the sim moving its own cursor, and the durations this was designed against were measured on one fast machine (maintainer, 2026-10-12). So each step is gated on what telemetry reads back and never on a delay, every measured duration is taken as a lower bound, and each timeout is a give-up that leaves the replay paused where it is. The website section says it in user terms, in the wording Frame Jog's section settles on (#1317): fast turns ride on iRacing's replay seeking, are less exact than slow ones, and vary with the machine and the length of the replay. A command from another key or dial that arrives while one of these modes is moving the replay is #1430's to hold, to let take over or to drop, by the one gate every replay sender goes through; these modes add none of their own.

**The two limits of the paced search are stated on the website** (coordinator, 2026-10-13), in the Replay Control page's dial section and in plain words; the wording is shown to the maintainer in full when it is written. What it has to say: a turn past the end of a list (no further lap, session or incident that way) does nothing on a replay playing at normal speed or slower, and on a paused replay, or after the first step of a spin, the dial stops after a few seconds; and a step to something more than about an hour and a half of recording away is given up the same way unless it is the first step of a turn made on a replay playing at normal speed or slower, while the key reaches it in every case.

## Out of scope

- **Making the record**, and the keypad's use of it (#1426).
- **Incident steps from the record** (#1426's follow-up).
- **A car-number or race-position mode.** Camera Controls' dial has both.
- **Next / Previous Green** as a stepping target (#1354's designed extension).
- **Changing what the keypad's searches do to playback.**
- **A setting** for the search limit, the pacing or the patience. The patience's floor is the walk's wait, which the Fastest Lap Search Delay setting already tunes for a slow machine; its cap and the hold are fixed.
- **A paced search further away than the patience**, other than as the first step on a playing replay, and every step of a run on the playing replay ([#1439](https://github.com/niklam/iracedeck/issues/1439), _The paced search_).
- **The keypad's car keys and Camera Controls' dial during a jump.** Their taps go out at once as today and are lost while a seek runs. That is #1435 and is not folded in here.
- **Ulanzi.** No Ulanzi dial is declared until its dials are verified (rule 7).

**Manual, in an iRacing replay**, on a Stream Deck+ and on the Mirabox knob:

2. **Lap, replay recorded without the plugin:** single detents pace through iRacing's search, and a spin of four lands four laps on with the caption counting down. 3. **Session** in a multi-session event: the same two cases. Then, in a replay recorded without the plugin whose sessions start about an hour apart, a spin of two: it must land two sessions on, and never part-way. 4. **Incident:** step through several, and spin past the last one; the dial must stop cleanly at the end of the recording within about seven seconds, wherever in the recording the last incident lies, and a Play key pressed straight after must act at once.
