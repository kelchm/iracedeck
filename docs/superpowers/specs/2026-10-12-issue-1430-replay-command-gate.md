# One gate for every replay command: only a jump goes into a jump under way

> **Issue:** [#1430](https://github.com/niklam/iracedeck/issues/1430) · **Supersedes:** _none_ · **Superseded by:** _none_
>
> Point-in-time design record. The code and `.claude/rules/` are the truth; this is not documentation.

A replay command sent while a position jump is still under way ends the jump somewhere else, and today every key and dial sends at once. This spec puts one gate in front of every replay command the plugin sends. It builds on the jump sequence of #1276 (`2026-09-28-issue-1276-replay-jump-playback.md`) and on the pending target Frame Jog introduced (#1317, in `2026-10-04-issue-1316-replay-control-dial.md`), and it is the gate that #1426 (`2026-10-12-issue-1426-replay-record-steps.md`) and #801 (`2026-10-12-issue-801-replay-control-dial-stepping.md`) both call for: they build none of their own.

## The decision

1. **One module sends every replay command.** `shared/replay-command-gate.ts` is the only caller of `getCommands().replay` in the plugin, and a test refuses any other. A surface says what it wants sent; the gate decides when it goes out, and whether.
2. **One record of where the replay is headed.** Every position jump the plugin sends is recorded as the pending target, whichever surface sent it. It is #1317's rule moved to `shared/`, and it replaces the two records there are today: the dial's pending target and the replay cursor's pending landing.
3. **While something is under way, only a position jump goes out**: an absolute one, go to start or go to end. A frame step becomes an absolute jump, a pause sends nothing, and a playing speed or a search waits for the landing to read back (the table below). The same holds behind a search that has not landed, where a pause waits too (amended 2026-10-13, after the captures).
4. **A command that waits is sent once or dropped, never sent late.** It goes out when the landing has read back. It is dropped, with a debug line and nothing sent, when the jump ends anywhere but on its target or when three seconds have passed since the press. Holding the press until the jump lands was chosen over dropping it (maintainer, 2026-10-12).
5. **Every wait ends on what telemetry reads back.** No measured duration is a constant that correctness rests on (maintainer, 2026-10-12): the durations below are one fast machine's, and each clock here is a give-up that sends nothing.

## The sim facts this rests on

All measured on 2026-10-12 and 2026-10-13 by a scripted probe and by the real dial code against the maintainer's sim (a saved replay of 204563 frames in three sessions, one fast machine, so every duration here is a lower bound for a slower one). The full record is in #1426's spec and in the header of `replay-position-state.ts` (#1317); these are the ones the gate is built on. The last seven were measured on 2026-10-13 and are what the four captures under _What the captures showed_ found.

- **Any replay command but a position jump, sent into a position jump under way, ends the jump where it stands.** A frame step (1684 to 8768 frames off its target), a lap, session or incident search (on the start frame, half-way, and five frames short), a play command (about 9000 frames on), and a pause sent alone 170 ms into a jump (about 8700 frames on, 3 of 3), even on a replay that is already paused.
- **An absolute position jump sent meanwhile retargets the seek, and the newest target wins** (10 of 10; fifty jumps 60 ms apart landed on the last one's frame). A jump relative to the cursor is resolved from the start of the tape.
- **A seek shows in telemetry only from about 20 ms after the send**, as `IsReplayPlaying` false and `ReplayFrameNum` 0 until the cursor lands. `ReplayPlaySpeed` reads 0 throughout (223 of 223 mid-seek samples, all on a paused replay). The first sample that shows the target frame is back in the replay.
- **A spin that keeps retargeting reads "in a replay" on an older target for a tick or two about every 400 ms**, while a newer jump has been sent and does not show yet. So a reading that is out of a seek does not mean nothing is under way.
- **A jump sent within about 415 ms of another's landing is carried out about 415 ms after that landing**, at any depth of the tape; a lone jump of tens of thousands of frames forward takes about half a second.
- **A search after quiet shows nothing on its way**: the old frame until the new one, no frame 0 and no drop of the flag. Two searches on their way at once do not add up; one sent after the last has read back does.
- **A search sent in the poll that first reads a landing can be lost**: 3 of 3 after a long jump, where one sent 100 ms or more later landed 6 of 6. Nothing between was measured.
- **A speed sent on the first read of a landing is acted on**: the dial's held Play, 6 of 6, and #1276's playback step.
- **A camera switch does not end a seek** (6 of 6, the broadcast).
- **In the maintainer's hardware round** (Stream Deck+, the same day) a Replay Control key in Play / Pause mode pressed about 50 ms after the last jump of a Frame Jog spin threw the replay twice: headed for frame 15811 it read 24530, and headed for 24642 it read 33291, playing. The dial's own Press in the same situation held the Play and landed exactly.
- **A search is a seek that telemetry does not show, and it is on its way for as long as its distance takes.** A lap search of 475 frames landed within about 20 ms, one of 6774 frames after about 68 ms, a session search of 22375 frames after about 195 ms (each on the same frame and within a few ms of that on every lone run, 21 in all). That is iRacing's own limit of about 2048 frames a sim update, which the 60000-frame position jump fits too (490 ms). On a paused replay nothing says a search is on its way: of 335 telemetry variables recorded through one, only the six that change on a quiet replay anyway changed before the landing. On a playing replay the frame stands still until it lands.
- **A pause, a Play or a frame step sent into a search on its way cancels the search**: 12 of 12 into the session search (50 and 100 ms after it) and 12 of 12 into the 6774-frame lap search (20 and 40 ms after it), each from a clean start, with the lone search right again between the groups (14 of 14). Sent in the search's first sim update the newer command simply replaces it and is acted on. Sent later it is not acted on either: Play read speed 1 with the frame standing still for the 1.5 s it was left (6 of 6), the frame step did not count (6 of 6), and a further frame step and a second of Play moved nothing (the one time it was tried). A position jump put it right every time. Left alone instead, the next search landed 1436 to 6444 frames past its frame 5 times in 8.
- **An absolute position jump sent into a search on its way wins**: it landed on its own frame 8 of 8, and the search was gone.
- **A search with nothing to find leaves nothing on its way.** Next Session in the last session: a pause, a Play, a frame step and a jump 50 and 100 ms later were each acted on as on a quiet replay (16 of 16). So the harm is the seek's, not the search command's.
- **Go to start and go to end are position jumps.** Each shows a seek and lands in about 30 ms alone. Sent 20, 100 and 200 ms into a position jump under way, go to end landed on the end 10 of 10 and go to start on frame 0, 6 of 6. On a saved replay, where the end is a frame of the tape (about 60 short of its last, here); Jump to Live in a live session is not measured.
- **Two position commands inside about one sim update do not always end on the newer.** An absolute jump 35, 60 and 200 ms after go to start or go to end landed on its own frame 20 of 20; 20 ms after, 5 of 10; 5 ms after, 0 of 6. It is the hole already known for two absolute jumps, and the target's read-back or its give-up is what catches it.
- **A pause and then an absolute jump on a playing replay lands on the jump's frame and rests paused**: 57 of 57, at 1x, 4x and 16x, for jumps of 3, 5000 and 60000 frames forward and 5000 back, with the jump sent 0.7, 17 and 35 ms after the pause, before the pause had read back in each back-to-back case and in some of the others.

## What counts as under way

The gate answers one question for every sender: is the plugin moving the replay right now. Five things say yes.

- **A pending target stands.** Recorded when a position jump goes out, and ended when its frame reads back from a replay in a way that can only mean the move is done (`conclusive`, as #1317 defined it: a frame the replay could not have been showing anyway, or a reading that was not the replay's seen since the send). The first out-of-seek reading does not end it, for the reason in the sim facts.
- **A target that never reads back is given up**: for as long as the jump that sent it holds the replay cursor it stands (the bound is the #1276 sequence's own wait, whose clock stops while a seek shows), and otherwise `REPLAY_TARGET_GIVE_UP_MS` (3 s) after the send, as on the dial today. Not a clock that stops while a seek shows: a replay left for the garage reads as a seek, and a target kept through it would send the next turn from a frame that is minutes old.
- **A replay jump that knows its frame is under way from the press, not from its seek** (added 2026-10-12, found reading #1433 against #1276). The #1276 sequence pauses a moving replay and waits for the pause to show before it seeks, a poll or more. In that window nothing has gone out that a target or telemetry could show, and the replay reads paused on a valid frame: a Frame Jog detent there steps one frame and takes the cursor, the sequence is cancelled before its seek, and the marker or fastest-lap jump is lost. So the sequence records the frame it is going to as the target when it starts, before its pause. Such a target cannot read back until its seek has gone out, and what the replay "could not have been showing anyway" is judged from that send, with the replay paused, not from the press: a replay fast-forwarding at 16x covers the 60 frames to the nearest marker in the time the pause takes to show, and a frame it merely ran across is not a landing. The fastest-lap walk's probes have no frame to record; a command during them ends the walk, as now.
- **A search the gate sent has not landed.** Telemetry cannot show one, so the gate records it, until its landing reads back or its landing wait runs out. Go to start and go to end are recorded the same way: they are position jumps, but to a frame the gate does not know. **Landed** is one judgement, the one the gate exports below: the frame moved by more than playback explains, then two out-of-seek reads 50 ms apart that agree within what playback explains. **The landing wait** is the caller's bound; for a key the gate works it out from the reading at the send (amended 2026-10-13): the frames between the cursor and the edge of the tape the search runs towards, at iRacing's 2048 frames a sim update and 60 updates a second, doubled, and never under 200 ms. A search cannot be on its way for longer than its distance takes, so this is the one bound that covers a far search in a long recording, which the fastest-lap walk's wait (1.6 s at its default) does not. It is a model and not a measurement of anyone else's machine: the doubling is the margin, and a sim that falls further behind than that fails as the plugin does today.
- **The latest reading shows a seek** (`isSeeking`: `IsReplayPlaying` false and nobody driving) while the debounced replay state still says a replay is on screen. This is the witness for a seek nobody recorded. Past the debounce's grace a seek and the garage read the same, and nothing is held on it.

A jump to the frame a replay reading already shows, sent with no target standing, is sent and records no target: it can never read back conclusively, and everything behind it would wait three seconds and be dropped. A marker key on the marker the replay stands on and a second Jump to Fastest Lap are the cases.

The target is cleared only by its read-back, its give-up, a newer jump replacing it, or the exit of a jump whose seek was never sent (below). Today any other action's command clears it and is then sent into the jump; that pair of steps is the bug.

## What each command does

| Command | Nothing under way | A target stands, or a seek shows | A search has not landed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Absolute position jump | sent, target recorded | sent, replaces the target | sent, target recorded, the search forgotten |
| Go to start, go to end (Jump to Live) | sent, recorded as under way | sent, the target cleared, recorded as under way | waits |
| Frame step | sent; on a paused replay the target is recorded one frame on | becomes an absolute jump one frame from the target | waits |
| Pause | sent | **not sent** | waits |
| Play and every other speed | sent | waits | waits |
| Search (lap, session, incident) | sent, recorded as under way | waits, and the landing must hold | waits |
| Anything else (`searchSessionTime`, `eraseTape`, a relative jump) | sent | waits, and the landing must hold | waits |

The third column was different before the captures: a pause and a speed were sent into a search that had not landed, on the model that neither harms one. Both cancel it (the sim facts above), so both wait. Go to start and go to end had been modelled as searches and waited behind a jump; they retarget it, so they go out at once.

- **A frame step into a jump is Frame Jog's own rule**, now for every sender: `planFrameMove` over the shared target, so a Frame Forward key, its long-press repeat and a dial detent are one code path with one end-of-tape margin. When only the witness says a seek is under way there is no frame to measure from, and the step is dropped. The key's repeat waits 250 ms after each step, so a held key on a quiet replay keeps sending real steps.
- **A pause sends nothing into a jump because the replay is already paused.** Every jump the plugin makes pauses a moving replay before it seeks (the #1276 sequence, Frame Jog). The press still takes the replay cursor, which ends a jump sequence's playback step and empties the waiting slot, so the replay rests paused on the target. The same holds while a replay jump (`beginReplayJump`: a sequence, the fastest-lap walk, #801's search runner) holds the cursor having paused the replay: the pause ends it and sends nothing.
- **A speed waits for the target's read-back and goes out on that reading.** Measured safe on the first read.
- **Behind a search that has not landed, a speed, a pause and a frame step wait for the search to land** (amended 2026-10-13). Each of the three cancels a search on its way, and from the second sim update on is not acted on either. The pause waits here, where into a jump it is not sent, because a key's search does not pause a playing replay: the pause is still owed when the search lands, and one sent to a replay that is already paused then is harmless (6 of 6). They go out when the search has landed in the gate's one sense of the word, which is 50 ms after the first reading that shows it: sent from 30 ms after a landing on, each of the three was acted on 6 of 6. If the landing wait runs out first, the search found nothing or was lost, nothing is on its way any more, and the held command goes out as on a quiet replay, which is what was measured after a search with nothing to find. If three seconds pass first, it is dropped like any other waiting command. The cost is the search with nothing to find: Next Session in the last session, half-way through this tape, holds a Play pressed straight after it for up to 1.7 s, and deep in a long recording the three-second drop comes first. A search that finds something lands in 20 to 200 ms here, and the Play pressed during it now plays from there.
- **A search waits for the read-back and then for the reading to hold**: three reads 50 ms apart, the read-back form of the 100 ms that worked every time where the first read lost the search. This is #801's rule, and its only home is here.
- **A command that waits takes the replay cursor at the press, as every command does today.** A jump sequence under way sends nothing more, so a Play pressed during a fastest-lap walk ends the walk and does not wait for it. A speed or a pause pressed during a jump is the driver's own word and replaces the sequence's playback step, which is #1276's rule unchanged.
- **A sequence still in its pause always gets a seek** (added 2026-10-12, amended 2026-10-13). A sequence whose claim is taken sends nothing more today, which is right for what follows its landing and wrong for a seek it has not sent yet: a Play pressed 30 ms after Next Marker on a playing replay would lose the jump, and the same Play 100 ms later would not. So whatever replay command takes the cursor in that window, a seek goes out. When the taker is a position jump or a frame step turned into one, it is the taker's own: sent at once like any jump into a jump under way, with no second pause, and it settles what the sequence owed, so nothing more goes out when the pause reads back. That was the third capture: a jump sent up to 35 ms after its pause, before the pause had read back, landed on its frame and rested paused 57 of 57, at 1x, 4x and 16x and across 60000 frames. When the taker sends no position jump (a press the gate holds, a pause it does not send, or a replay jump that waits on the target, which #801's search runner is and the gate's own jump for a waiting search is), one seek goes out to the target that stands once the pause has read back, as the sequence does it today: to the sequence's own frame, or to a newer one if a later jump has moved the target meanwhile and not yet sent its own. The sequence's own wait for its pause is kept as built; the captures say it could seek at once too, and that is a change to #1276's sequence this fix does not need. The taker owns only the playback step. The caller's own step after the landing, Jump to Fastest Lap's camera switch, still runs when the seek went to the sequence's frame: it belongs to where the jump is going, and a camera switch was measured safe. A target whose seek is never sent is cleared at that exit by whoever holds the seek, which after a takeover in the pause window is the taker and not the sequence that sent the pause: cancelled before it ran, the pause refused or never showing, the seek itself refused, or the claim taken by a key that sends no replay command. The give-up clocks start at a send, and here there is none.
- **A car or camera key is outside the gate.** It sends no replay command, the Next Car key does not harm a jump (the fourth capture: 9 of 9 on their frames), and what it does to a jump is as built: it takes the cursor to stop a fastest-lap walk searching for another car, a sequence it cancels in its pause sends no seek, and one it cancels during its seek lands with no playback step, which is #1428 and is not changed here. The jump's own camera switch is not sent after it: the driver's camera choice is the newer one.
- **A search that waits behind a jump sequence carries that jump's playback on.** Otherwise the same key pressed slowly would leave the replay playing and pressed quickly would leave it paused, and #1426's commonest miss is exactly that: two record jumps toward the end of what was recorded, then a press the record cannot answer. So the waiting search inherits the speed the sequence captured, as a jump that takes over from a jump does today, and when the search has landed, or its landing wait has run out on a search with nothing to find, the gate applies the **Replay jump playback** step. Search first and playback after: a search on the held reading of a paused landing is the measured case. If the search is dropped as stale, the step goes out when the jump lands on its target; a jump that ends off its target sends nothing more, as now. A search pressed on a quiet replay is unchanged: no pause and no playback step. Behind a dial's frame move there is no captured speed and nothing to carry.
- **A claim holder's own commands go through the gate too.** The jump sequence's pause is not sent while a target stands (today's takeover rule, now also true when the jump under way is a dial's), its seek is a jump, and its playback step goes out after its own read-back wait.

## The waiting slot

One command waits at a time, for the whole plugin, and the newest wins: a second Play replaces the first, a search replaces a waiting speed. A pause empties it, and behind a search that has not landed it then waits in it itself. So does a new jump, which is a newer word about where the replay should be: a waiting search was meant to start from the old target, and a frame move is a request to stand still. That holds for every replay jump (`beginReplayJump`), whatever it sends first, #801's search runner included, whose first send is a pause or a search and not a position jump. A jump that starts while a speed waits takes that speed as the one it captured, so the **Replay jump playback** setting decides what becomes of it, and the speed is not sent on the read-back the jump is itself waiting for.

The slot is checked on a 20 ms timer (`REPLAY_JUMP_POLL_MS`) that runs only while something waits. It decides one thing: send or drop. A dropped command writes a debug line saying what was dropped and why, and the key's icon follows telemetry as before.

**Three seconds** (`REPLAY_TARGET_GIVE_UP_MS`, one number for both) is the longest a key press waits. A press that fires many seconds late is worse than one that is dropped. The slot and the three seconds are the same behind a search as behind a jump; the only difference is what ends the wait without a landing. Behind a jump that is a landing off target, and the command is dropped. Behind a search it is the landing wait running out, and the command is sent, because by then nothing is on its way.

## What it gives #1426 and #801

Two waits, exported from the gate, each taking its time bound from the caller and resolving settled, gave up, or superseded by another replay command:

- **Wait until settled, holding N reads.** Nothing under way, shown on N reads 50 ms apart.
- **Wait for a search to land.** The frame moved by more than playback explains, then two out-of-seek reads 50 ms apart that agree within what playback explains. A timeout means not landed; an unmoved search and a lost one look the same, and the caller decides whether to try again. The test is unchanged by the captures. What they add is what the bound is for: a speed, a pause or a frame step sent before this wait has ended cancels a search that is still on its way and leaves the frame standing, so a caller that sends one after a timeout needs a bound that covers the search's distance. The gate exports the one it uses for keys (the frames to the edge of the tape in the search's direction, at 2048 a sim update, doubled) for a caller to take or to exceed.

Policy stays with the caller. #801's runner keeps its signed count, its clamp of ten, its one retry and its playback step, built on the two waits, and is not cut off by the keys' three seconds. #1426's Lap and Session keys step from the shared target while it stands and from the debounced frame otherwise, and its miss path sends a search through the gate like any other key. Both specs are amended to say so.

## The seam, and what it costs

About 73 send sites in seven files change from `getCommands().replay.x()` to the gate. The files touched:

- **New:** `shared/replay-command-gate.ts` and its test; the single-sender test.
- **Moved to `shared/`:** `replay-control/replay-position-state.ts` and its test, and `planFrameMove` out of `replay-control-frame-jog.ts`. `keepReplayTarget` goes: the dial's commands no longer need to protect the target from the dial's own cursor takes.
- **`shared/replay-cursor.ts`:** the pending landing (`recordReplayLanding`, `pendingReplayLanding`, `clearReplayLanding`) is replaced by the shared target; a cursor take no longer clears it. The claim and the pending jump speed are unchanged.
- **`shared/replay-jump-playback.ts`:** sends through the gate; `tookOver` reads "something is under way" from it. Claiming the cursor clears the pending jump speed, which is why `beginReplayJump` reads it first; the gate's jump for a waiting search has to read it before its own claim too. The sequence and the target must share one judgement of "landed": today the sequence takes the exact frame from a sample that is in a replay, and the target takes a conclusive read-back. Kept as two, the sequence can read its landing a tick before the target ends, and its own playback speed would then meet a target still standing and be held as a waiting speed, with `applyReplayJumpPlayback`'s result no longer true. So the sequence's landing wait is the gate's read-back.
- **`replay-control.ts`:** 42 sites in `executeMode`, the fastest-lap walk and the key repeat.
- **`replay-control-dial-surface.ts`:** `heldSpeed`, `isPausedForMove` and the follow timer's send move into the gate; the surface keeps its redraw.
- **`replay-markers-ops.ts`:** its jump records the shared target and its anchor reads it. The marker surfaces' one-second, 60-frame rule for when a landing still anchors is replaced by the target's read-back.
- **The hidden legacy actions** Replay Transport (8 sites), Replay Speed (3) and Replay Navigation (11): the same swap. They are still runnable from older profiles, so they are not left out.
- **Tests** for each of the above, which mock `getCommands` today and will mock or drive the gate.
- **Docs:** `.claude/rules/encoders-and-touchscreen.md`, `packages/iracing-actions/CLAUDE.md`, the Replay Control and Replay Markers website pages, and a Bug Fixes changelog fragment (a key thrown by a Replay Markers jump shipped before the dial did).

Two open issues become one call from the gate each, and neither is made here. #1429 (Replay Markers treats a long seek as the replay being left) wants the SDK's replay state told about the plugin's own seeks, and the gate knows every one of them. #1427 wants one pending speed for keys, dial and jumps, and the gate sends every speed, a held one included: until it records them, a marker jump begun within a poll or two of a Play going out reads speed 0 and ends paused under Remember state, as it does today for a plain Play key.

## What the user sees

- A key or dial input made while the replay is still jumping acts when the jump lands, about 0.4 s later on the maintainer's machine, instead of throwing the replay thousands of frames.
- A Frame Forward or Frame Backward press during a jump moves one frame from where the jump is going.
- A Pause during a jump leaves the replay paused on the frame the jump was going to.
- A Play, a Pause or a frame step pressed straight after a Next Lap, Session or Incident press acts when that search has landed, where today it cancels the search and can leave the replay reading as playing with the picture standing still. After a search with nothing to find it acts a moment later, up to three seconds deep in a long recording.
- Jump to Live and Go to start pressed during a jump go out at once and win.
- A Next Lap, Session or Incident press during a marker or fastest-lap jump moves on from where that jump lands, and the replay then plays or stays paused as the **Replay jump playback** setting says, the same as when the two are pressed slowly.
- If the jump takes longer than three seconds, or does not land on its frame, the waiting press does nothing.

The website says it in the wording Frame Jog's section settled on: quick inputs ride on iRacing's own replay seeking, and take longer on a slower machine or in a long replay.

## What the captures showed

Four captures were not taken when this was written: the sim was down, and the spec went first (maintainer, 2026-10-12). Each was modelled, with what a different result would change. They were taken on 2026-10-13 on the tape and machine named under the sim facts, by the scripted probe, every case from a quiet paused replay unless it says playing. One model held, two changed the table, and one let a wait go.

- **Play, pause and a frame step after a search.** Modelled: a speed and a pause do not harm a search on its way; a step does. **Wrong for the speed and the pause.** Sent 50 to 150 ms after a lap search of 475 frames, as the capture was worded, all three were harmless (18 of 18), but only because that search had landed inside 20 ms. Into a search still on its way all three cancel it: 12 of 12 into a session search of 22375 frames and 12 of 12 into a lap search of 6774. Sent 0.7 ms after the 475-frame search the search was lost 6 of 6, at 5 ms 4 of 6, at 10 ms 0 of 6. So the third column of the table now holds all three. The spec had said a pause there would become "not sent"; it waits instead, for the reason under the table.
- **Go to end sent into a jump under way.** Modelled as a search that waits. **It retargets the seek**: 10 of 10 on the end, and go to start 6 of 6 on frame 0. Both now go out at once.
- **A pause and then an absolute jump at once on a playing replay.** Modelled as unsafe until the pause has read back. **Safe**: 57 of 57. A taker's jump in a sequence's pause window now goes out at once.
- **The Next Car key sent into a jump.** Modelled as safe, never passing through the gate. **Safe for the jump**: 9 of 9 on their frames (the key 20, 100 and 200 ms into a 60000-frame jump, and 100 ms into the slow path of a near pair). The tap itself did nothing: the camera stayed on its car in all 9, where the same tap on a quiet replay moved it 2 of 2. A car key pressed during a jump is lost today and stays lost here (_Out of scope_).

**The control for the fix** (Testing, step 1) was run the same day on `master` at `d251b7020`, through the merged dial code: a Frame Jog spin of 16 frames, then 50 ms after its last jump the Replay Control key's command. All eight were thrown. Play, the pause and the frame step each ended 7597 frames past the spin's target, twice each, the Play playing from there; Next Lap ended on frame 102289 twice, where the same search alone found 102769.

Still unmeasured, and covered by the read-backs and not by any number here: a slower machine; Jump to Live in a live session; go to start or go to end sent into a search on its way (modelled: it waits); a speed on the very first reading of a search's landing (the gate waits 50 ms more); and anything between 0 and 100 ms after a jump's landing is read.

## Out of scope

- **Commands from iRacing's own replay bar or keyboard.** The plugin cannot see them; the target's give-up and the witness are what limit the damage.
- **A car or camera key pressed during a jump.** The tap is swallowed while the seek runs (the fourth capture). The gate's settled wait is what a fix would use, and it is not made here.
- **Making key searches add up.** With one waiting slot three quick Next Lap presses move two laps, never part-way. A count is #801's runner.
- **The record steps and the search runner themselves** (#1426, #801).
- **Telling the SDK's replay state about the plugin's seeks** (#1429).
- **The step-per-sim-update limit**: two frame steps inside one sim update still count as one.
- **A setting** for the three seconds or the hold.
- **Ulanzi dials**, which are not declared.

## Testing

**Unit:**

- **The gate:** each cell of the table, by command kind and by what is under way; a waiting speed goes out on the first reading that ends the target and a waiting search only on the third held read; behind a search that has not landed a Play, a pause and a frame step each wait, go out once the moved frame has held for two reads 50 ms apart, go out when the landing wait ends on a search that moved nothing, and are dropped at three seconds if neither came first; the landing wait is longer the further the edge of the tape is in the search's direction, and never under 200 ms; go to start and go to end are sent into a standing target and clear it; an absolute jump sent behind a search forgets the search; newest wins in the slot; a pause empties it and sends nothing; a jump empties it, and a jump sequence that starts while a speed waits captures that speed; a drop at three seconds and on a landing off target, each with nothing sent; nothing held outside a replay or past the debounce's grace.
- **The target:** the moved `replay-position-state` tests pass unchanged; a cursor take no longer clears it; it stands while its jump holds the cursor and for three seconds otherwise; a jump to the frame on screen records none.
- **Each sender:** Replay Control's Play / Pause, a speed mode, a frame step, the repeat and a search, each pressed with a marker jump, a fastest-lap record jump and a dial jump under way; the dial's Speed Control detent, Press and Jump to Live with a marker jump under way; a Frame Jog detent on a replay-true reading with another surface's jump just sent; the three legacy actions. In every case: nothing but a position jump is sent while the target stands.
- **The jump sequence:** its pause is not sent when a dial jump is under way; a speed pressed during it goes out once, at the landing; a search pressed during it goes out after the landing has held and is followed by the playback step with the speed the sequence captured, under each value of the setting; a search dropped as stale still gets the playback step on the landing, and none when the jump ends off its target; a replay jump that starts while a speed waits captures it and the speed is not sent, for a position jump and for one whose first send is a pause; with the sequence's pause sent and not yet shown: a Play, a pause, a search and a replay jump that sends no position jump each leave one seek to go out to the sequence's frame once the pause shows, and then do what the table says; a second jump and a Frame Jog detent each move the target and send their seek at once with no second pause, and nothing more goes out when the pause shows; a pause that never shows, a pause refused, a sequence cancelled before it ran and a seek refused each clear the target and leave nothing waiting on it; a held Play and then a second marker press in that window end in one seek, to the second marker's frame; a target recorded before its seek is not ended by the replay running across its frame before the seek goes out; with a held Play behind Jump to Fastest Lap the camera still switches after the landing, and with a car key as the canceller it does not; a car key that cancels a sequence in its pause leaves no target standing; a search pressed during a jump that finds nothing still gets the playback step when its landing wait ends.
- **The single-sender test** fails on a planted `getCommands().replay` call outside the gate.
- **The two waits:** settled, gave up and superseded for each; the bound is the caller's.

**The real code against the sim** (the harness used for #1317, with the observer in a child process):

1. **Before the fix, as the control:** a Frame Jog spin, then 50 ms after its last jump the Replay Control key's Play, its pause, a frame step and a lap search, each as the key sends it. Each should throw the replay, the Play by about 8700 frames as in the maintainer's round. A control that does not fail proves nothing about step 2.
2. **After the fix, the same four:** each lands on the spin's target, then plays, rests, stands one frame on, or stands one lap on.
3. **The other way round:** a marker jump across the tape, then a Speed Control detent, a Press and a Frame Jog detent 20, 50 and 200 ms later.
4. **The whole logged minute of the maintainer's Frame Jog round** again at three depths of the tape, which must still act on every detent.
5. **The four captures**, taken on 2026-10-13 (_What the captures showed_).
6. **After the fix, a search and then a key:** a Next Session press across the tape and, 50 and 100 ms later, the key's Play, its pause and a frame step. Each must end on the session's first frame, playing, paused, or one frame on. The same three after Next Session in the last session must act once the landing wait has run out.

**Manual, in an iRacing replay**, on a Stream Deck+ and on the Mirabox knob:

1. Spin Frame Jog and press a Replay Control key in Play / Pause mode straight after: the replay lands where the spin was going and then plays.
2. The same with Frame Forward, with a held Frame Forward, with Next Lap and with Next Incident.
3. Press Next Marker and then Play / Pause on another key before the picture has moved; then Pause instead. Do both on a playing replay too, as fast as the two keys can be pressed, and with a Frame Jog detent in place of the key: the replay must always end at the marker, or one frame from it for the detent.
4. With the replay playing and **Replay jump playback** on Remember state, press Next Marker and then Next Incident at once: the replay must be playing after it lands, as it is when the two are pressed slowly. Again with the setting on Stopped.
5. Press Jump to Fastest Lap and turn a Speed Control dial during the walk: the walk stops and the speed is obeyed.
6. Press a marker key for the marker the replay stands on, then Play at once: it plays without a wait.
7. From early in a session, press Next Session and then Play / Pause as fast as the two keys can be pressed: the replay must land on the next session and play. Then Next Session in the last session and Play straight after: it plays, a moment later.
8. Press Jump to Live during a marker jump: the replay ends at the end of the recording.
7. An old profile's Replay Transport and Replay Navigation keys, pressed during a marker jump.
8. From the car: every key and dial does what it did before.
