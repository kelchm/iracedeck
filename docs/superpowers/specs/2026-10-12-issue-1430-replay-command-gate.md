# One gate for every replay command: only a jump goes into a jump under way

> **Issue:** [#1430](https://github.com/niklam/iracedeck/issues/1430) · **Supersedes:** _none_ · **Superseded by:** _none_
>
> Point-in-time design record. The code and `.claude/rules/` are the truth; this is not documentation.

A replay command sent while a position jump is still under way ends the jump somewhere else, and today every key and dial sends at once. This spec puts one gate in front of every replay command the plugin sends. It builds on the jump sequence of #1276 (`2026-09-28-issue-1276-replay-jump-playback.md`) and on the pending target Frame Jog introduced (#1317, in `2026-10-04-issue-1316-replay-control-dial.md`), and it is the gate that #1426 (`2026-10-12-issue-1426-replay-record-steps.md`) and #801 (`2026-10-12-issue-801-replay-control-dial-stepping.md`) both call for: they build none of their own.

## The decision

1. **One module sends every replay command.** `shared/replay-command-gate.ts` is the only caller of `getCommands().replay` in the plugin, and a test refuses any other. A surface says what it wants sent; the gate decides when it goes out, and whether.
2. **One record of where the replay is headed.** Every position jump the plugin sends is recorded as the pending target, whichever surface sent it. It is #1317's rule moved to `shared/`, and it replaces the two records there are today: the dial's pending target and the replay cursor's pending landing.
3. **While something is under way, only a position jump goes out**: an absolute one, go to start or go to end. A frame step becomes an absolute jump, a pause sends nothing, and a playing speed or a search waits for the landing to read back (the table below). The same holds behind a search that may be on its way, where a pause waits too (amended 2026-10-13, after the captures).
4. **A command that waits goes out when what it waits on has landed, however long that takes** (amended 2026-10-13). Behind a position jump it is dropped, with a debug line and nothing sent, only when the jump ends anywhere but on its target or the jump's own wait runs out. Behind a search, which telemetry cannot show on a paused replay, a second press is the way out: it takes over at once, by a position jump that is right whether or not a search was running. Holding the press until the jump lands was chosen over dropping it (maintainer, 2026-10-12), and a wait with no cap over one of three seconds (maintainer, 2026-10-13).
5. **Every wait ends on what telemetry reads back.** No measured duration is a constant that correctness rests on (maintainer, 2026-10-12): the durations below are one fast machine's, and each clock here is a give-up. It drops a press, or hands it to a takeover that is right either way; it never sends a bare command into something that may still be moving.
6. **It is built and tested for recordings of four to six hours** (maintainer, 2026-10-13: such sessions "are not that uncommon, so we need to be prepared for those"). Six hours is 1296000 frames, about 10.5 s of seeking from end to end at the measured rate. A 24-hour recording must not break and may be slow; nothing about one is measured.
7. **The gate's state can be read, and reading it changes nothing** (added 2026-10-13): _The state seam_, which the key indicator of #1437 is built on.

## The sim facts this rests on

All measured on 2026-10-12 and 2026-10-13 by a scripted probe and by the real dial code against the maintainer's sim (a saved replay of an official race on Motegi's full road course, SubSessionID 89265205: Practice, Lone Qualify and Race with a rolling start, 204563 frames; one fast machine, so every duration here is a lower bound for a slower one). The full record is in #1426's spec and in the header of `replay-position-state.ts` (#1317); these are the ones the gate is built on. Everything from the search facts on was measured on 2026-10-13 (_What the captures showed_).

- **Any replay command but a position jump, sent into a position jump under way, ends the jump where it stands.** A frame step (1684 to 8768 frames off its target), a lap, session or incident search (on the start frame, half-way, and five frames short), a play command (about 9000 frames on), and a pause sent alone 170 ms into a jump (about 8700 frames on, 3 of 3), even on a replay that is already paused.
- **An absolute position jump sent meanwhile retargets the seek, and the newest target wins** (10 of 10; fifty jumps 60 ms apart landed on the last one's frame). A jump relative to the cursor is resolved from the start of the tape.
- **A seek shows in telemetry only from about 20 ms after the send**, as `IsReplayPlaying` false and `ReplayFrameNum` 0 until the cursor lands. `ReplayPlaySpeed` reads 0 throughout (223 of 223 mid-seek samples, all on a paused replay). The first sample that shows the target frame is back in the replay.
- **A spin that keeps retargeting reads "in a replay" on an older target for a tick or two about every 400 ms**, while a newer jump has been sent and does not show yet. So a reading that is out of a seek does not mean nothing is under way.
- **A jump sent within about 415 ms of another's landing is carried out about 415 ms after that landing**, at any depth of the tape; a lone jump of tens of thousands of frames forward takes about half a second.
- **A search after quiet shows nothing on its way**: the old frame until the new one, no frame 0 and no drop of the flag. Two searches on their way at once do not add up; one sent after the last has read back does.
- **A search sent in the poll that first reads a landing can be lost**: 3 of 3 after a long jump, where one sent 100 ms or more later landed 6 of 6. Nothing between was measured.
- **A speed sent on the first read of a landing is acted on**: the dial's held Play, 6 of 6, and #1276's playback step.
- **A camera switch does not end a seek** (6 of 6, the broadcast).
- **In the maintainer's hardware round** (Stream Deck+, the same day) a Replay Control key in Play / Pause mode pressed about 50 ms after the last jump of a Frame Jog spin threw the replay twice: headed for frame 15811 it read 24530, and headed for 24642 it read 33291, playing. The dial's own Press in the same situation held the Play and landed exactly.
- **A search is a seek that telemetry does not show, and it is on its way for as long as its distance takes.** A lap search of 475 frames landed within about 20 ms, one of 6774 frames after about 68 ms, a session search of 22375 frames after about 195 ms (each on the same frame and within a few ms of that on every lone run, 21 in all). That is iRacing's own limit of about 2048 frames a sim update, and position jumps keep to it as far as this tape reaches: 165, 400, 896 and 1495 ms for 30000, 60000, 120000 and 195000 frames from near the start of the tape, which from 60000 on is 2055 frames an update. Where a jump starts matters too: the same 60000 frames took 490 ms from the middle of the tape. On a paused replay nothing says a search is on its way: of 335 telemetry variables recorded through one, only the six that change on a quiet replay anyway changed before the landing. On a playing replay the frame stands still until it lands.
- **A pause, a Play or a frame step sent into a search on its way cancels the search**: 12 of 12 into the session search (50 and 100 ms after it) and 12 of 12 into the 6774-frame lap search (20 and 40 ms after it), each from a clean start, with the lone search right again between the groups (14 of 14). Sent in the search's first sim update the newer command simply replaces it and is acted on. Sent later it is not acted on either: Play read speed 1 with the frame standing still for the 1.5 s it was left (6 of 6), the frame step did not count (6 of 6), and a further frame step and a second of Play moved nothing (the one time it was tried). A position jump put it right every time. Left alone instead, the next search landed 1436 to 6444 frames past its frame 5 times in 8.
- **An absolute position jump sent into a search on its way wins**: it landed on its own frame 8 of 8, and the search was gone.
- **A search with nothing to find leaves nothing on its way.** Next Session in the last session: a pause, a Play, a frame step and a jump 50 and 100 ms later were each acted on as on a quiet replay (16 of 16). So the harm is the seek's, not the search command's.
- **Go to start and go to end are position jumps.** Each shows a seek and lands in about 30 ms alone. Sent 20, 100 and 200 ms into a position jump under way, go to end landed on the end 10 of 10 and go to start on frame 0, 6 of 6. On a saved replay, where the end is a frame of the tape (about 60 short of its last, here); Jump to Live in a live session is not measured.
- **Some frames cannot be landed on.** On this tape a position jump to any of frames 37809 to 37868, the last second of qualifying, rests on 37869, the race session's first frame, and stays there (measured by #1354's author: asked for 37843, 37818, 37811 and 37809, it read 37869 each time, while a jump to 37445 landed exactly). One boundary on one tape; the one between practice and qualifying was not looked at. The end of a tape is the other known case, where a jump rests up to 5 frames short (sent 204560 of 204563, it read 204558).
- **Two position commands inside about one sim update do not always end on the newer.** An absolute jump 35, 60 and 200 ms after go to start or go to end landed on its own frame 20 of 20; 20 ms after, 5 of 10; 5 ms after, 0 of 6. It is the hole already known for two absolute jumps, and the target's read-back or its give-up is what catches it.
- **A pause and then an absolute jump on a playing replay lands on the jump's frame and rests paused**: 57 of 57, at 1x, 4x and 16x, for jumps of 3, 5000 and 60000 frames forward and 5000 back, with the jump sent 0.7, 17 and 35 ms after the pause, before the pause had read back in each back-to-back case and in some of the others.
- **A camera change is dropped during a position jump's seek and not during a search.** Into a jump: the broadcast to another car left the camera where it was 3 of 3 and the Next Car key 9 of 9, the jump landing on its frame each time. Into the 22375-frame session search, 50 and 100 ms after it: the search landed 8 of 8, and the camera changed every time it could show, 5 of 5 (the broadcast and the key, four cases each). That is one search length on one fast machine.
- **A jump sequence started into a search on its way lands.** On a replay playing at 1x, 50 and 100 ms into that session search: a pause, a jump of 5000 frames and Play on the first reading of the landing ended on the jump's frame and then covered 60 frames in a second, 8 of 8, as the same sequence did with no search before it. Four had the pause read back before the seek, and four sent the two at once.
- **On `master` today a Frame Jog detent during a marker jump costs the jump or its playback**, by its timing (measured by #1354's author through the real dial and Replay Markers code). 46 ms after the Next Marker press, before the seek had gone out, the dial took the cursor and the seek was never sent. At 0 and about 30 ms after the seek, the seek landed and the replay rested paused where the setting said to play on.
- **On a playing replay a search on its way shows, and one with nothing to find does not.** On its way, the frame stands still until it leaps to the landing: 22 of 22 (the session search at 1x, 2x, 4x, 16x, reverse, half and quarter speed, a 6774-frame lap search and an incident search at 1x and 4x). It begins 9 to 64 ms after the send at 1x and faster and about 100 ms after at quarter speed, one to four frames having gone by. With nothing to find, the frame keeps advancing at playback's own pace: 14 of 14 at the same seven speeds. #1354's author saw one of each with a 5 ms sample.
- **A search clears slow motion.** A replay at half speed read speed 1 without the slow flag from the moment the frame stood still, and played on at 1x after the landing; one a step slower played on at 2x. 4 of 4.
- **A position jump to the frame beside the reading puts the replay right, into a search and after one** (the takeover, 14 of 14 on a paused replay). Sent 50 and 100 ms into the session search, one frame forward (4) and one back (1), it landed, and Play on its landing then covered 60 frames in a second as on a quiet replay. The same jump followed by a new search, once its landing had held 100 ms: the search landed on its frame, 4 of 4 after a far search and 1 of 1 after one with nothing to find. After a search with nothing to find, 100 ms and 1600 ms later: jump, then Play, 4 of 4.
- **Searches by their length, and go to start** (#1354's author, a 5 ms sample, paused): Next Session over 8406 frames 78 ms, over 29463 frames 250 ms; Previous Session over 166094 frames 1366 ms, which is 2027 frames a sim update. Go to start from the tape's end landed in 31 to 46 ms, twice, where a position jump over the same distance took 1601 ms: it is not a seek of that length. Previous Session from the race's first frame landed on frame 0. A search with nothing to find changed nothing in 2.5 s.
- **Nothing is measured past 195000 frames.** Six hours (1296000 frames, about 10.5 s end to end) and 24 hours (5184000 frames, about 42 s) are this tape's rate carried on. A recording that long is spooled to disk and may not seek at that rate at all.

## What counts as under way

The gate answers one question for every sender: is the plugin moving the replay right now. Five things say yes.

- **A pending target stands.** Recorded when a position jump goes out, and ended when its frame reads back from a replay in a way that can only mean the move is done (`conclusive`, as #1317 defined it: a frame the replay could not have been showing anyway, or a reading that was not the replay's seen since the send). The first out-of-seek reading does not end it, for the reason in the sim facts.
- **A target that never reads back is given up**: for as long as the jump that sent it holds the replay cursor it stands (the bound is the #1276 sequence's own wait, whose clock stops while a seek shows), and otherwise when its jump's own wait has run out, counted from the send: the larger of `REPLAY_TARGET_GIVE_UP_MS` (3 s, as on the dial today) and twice the jump's length at 2048 frames a sim update (amended 2026-10-13). Three seconds alone gives a target up with its seek still running for any jump longer than about 370000 frames, an hour and three quarters of recording, and a press held behind a marker jump takes the cursor from the sequence that would have kept the target standing. Not a clock that stops while a seek shows: a replay left for the garage reads as a seek, and a target kept through it would send the next turn from a frame that is minutes old. The length bounds that: a target outlives the replay being left by its jump's wait at the most. #1354's `replaySeekWaitMs` is the same model with the walk's floor, and the two are one function with the floor as its argument.
- **A replay jump that knows its frame is under way from the press, not from its seek** (added 2026-10-12, found reading #1433 against #1276). The #1276 sequence pauses a moving replay and waits for the pause to show before it seeks, a poll or more. In that window nothing has gone out that a target or telemetry could show, and the replay reads paused on a valid frame: a Frame Jog detent there steps one frame and takes the cursor, the sequence is cancelled before its seek, and the marker or fastest-lap jump is lost. So the sequence records the frame it is going to as the target when it starts, before its pause. Such a target cannot read back until its seek has gone out, and what the replay "could not have been showing anyway" is judged from that send, with the replay paused, not from the press: a replay fast-forwarding at 16x covers the 60 frames to the nearest marker in the time the pause takes to show, and a frame it merely ran across is not a landing. The fastest-lap walk's probes have no frame to record; a command during them ends the walk, as now.
- **A search the gate sent may be on its way.** Telemetry cannot show one on a paused replay, so the gate records it. Go to start and go to end are recorded the same way: they are position jumps, but to a frame the gate does not know. **Landed** is one judgement, the one the gate exports below: the frame moved by more than playback explains, then two out-of-seek reads 50 ms apart that agree within what playback explains. A search that found nothing and one still seeking look the same until then, so the gate keeps two things apart (amended 2026-10-13, after the maintainer's objection to a fixed limit on a long recording).
  - **How long the search is taken to be possibly on its way**: from its send until its landing reads back, or until it can no longer be running. That is the frames between the cursor and the edge of the tape the search runs towards, at iRacing's 2048 frames a sim update and 60 updates a second, doubled, and never under the fastest-lap walk's wait (the Fastest Lap Search Delay times four, 1.6 s at its default). The floor covers a search sent soon after another's landing, which iRacing carries out late whatever its distance (about 400 ms after the first landed, #1426's sim facts). From the start of a six-hour recording it is about 21 s.
  - **How long the record of it stands**: until a landing reads back, an absolute jump goes out (a takeover's included), or the replay is left. It has no time limit. While it stands, no speed, pause or frame step goes out bare.
  - So the time bound decides how long a press left alone goes on waiting. It never decides that a bare command is safe, and when the rate it is built on is wrong for a tape the cost is a press that takes over early or late, not a command sent into a seek.
  - **On a playing replay the gate reads it.** Once the frame has stood still for longer than playback explains, the search is seen to be on its way, and stays so until the frame leaps to the landing. If the frame is still advancing as playback explains one Fastest Lap Search Delay (400 ms at its default) after the send, the search found nothing and is no longer taken to be on its way. The record still stands, so those 400 ms are a patience and nothing correct rests on them. It is the same comparison of the frame with what playback explains that the landing judgement makes, with one more outcome.
- **The latest reading shows a seek** (`isSeeking`: `IsReplayPlaying` false and nobody driving) while the debounced replay state still says a replay is on screen. This is the witness for a seek nobody recorded. Past the debounce's grace a seek and the garage read the same, and nothing is held on it.

A target on a frame that cannot be landed on never reads back, and the gate has no second judgement for it: it stands for as long as the rule above says and is then given up, and a press held behind it is dropped with it. That is what a jump there does today (the sequence reports that it did not land and sends nothing more), and it fails safe. Taking a rest within a second past the target as its landing would let such a jump finish with its playback step, but it would rest on one boundary measured on one tape; it is not done here, and a second boundary measured is what would change that.

A jump to the frame a replay reading already shows, sent with no target standing, is sent and records no target: it can never read back conclusively, and everything behind it would wait out the target's give-up and be dropped. A marker key on the marker the replay stands on and a second Jump to Fastest Lap are the cases.

The target is cleared only by its read-back, its give-up, a newer jump replacing it, or the exit of a jump whose seek was never sent (below). Today any other action's command clears it and is then sent into the jump; that pair of steps is the bug.

## What each command does

| Command | Nothing under way | A target stands, or a seek shows | A search may be on its way |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Absolute position jump | sent, target recorded | sent, replaces the target | sent, target recorded, the search forgotten |
| Go to start, go to end (Jump to Live) | sent, recorded as under way | sent, the target cleared, recorded as under way | waits |
| Frame step | sent; on a paused replay the target is recorded one frame on | becomes an absolute jump one frame from the target | waits |
| Pause | sent | **not sent** | waits; nothing to send on a paused replay |
| Play and every other speed | sent | waits | waits |
| Search (lap, session, incident) | sent, recorded as under way | waits, and the landing must hold | waits |
| Anything else (`searchSessionTime`, `eraseTape`, a relative jump) | sent | waits, and the landing must hold | waits |

The third column was different before the captures: a pause and a speed were sent into a search that had not landed, on the model that neither harms one. Both cancel it (the sim facts above), so both wait. Go to start and go to end had been modelled as searches and waited behind a jump; they retarget it, so they go out at once. "Waits" in the third column has its own way out, which the second bullet below sets out.

- **A frame step into a jump is Frame Jog's own rule**, now for every sender: `planFrameMove` over the shared target, so a Frame Forward key, its long-press repeat and a dial detent are one code path with one end-of-tape margin. When only the witness says a seek is under way there is no frame to measure from, and the step is dropped. The key's repeat waits 250 ms after each step, so a held key on a quiet replay keeps sending real steps.
- **A pause sends nothing into a jump because the replay is already paused.** Every jump the plugin makes pauses a moving replay before it seeks (the #1276 sequence, Frame Jog). The press still takes the replay cursor, which ends a jump sequence's playback step and empties the waiting slot, so the replay rests paused on the target. The same holds while a replay jump (`beginReplayJump`: a sequence, the fastest-lap walk, #801's search runner) holds the cursor having paused the replay: the pause ends it and sends nothing. What decides it is the gate's own record of the pauses it has sent, not the reading and not who holds the cursor (added 2026-10-13): every pause goes through the gate, so it knows whether the replay has been paused for what is under way, and it knows so in the window where a pause has gone out and has not read back yet. A replay jump that holds the cursor without having paused, which #801's runner is while its first search is on its way on a playing replay, is no different from a key's search there: a Pause takes the cursor, waits for the landing and is sent.
- **A speed waits for the target's read-back and goes out on that reading.** Measured safe on the first read.
- **Behind a search that may be on its way, a press waits for the landing, and pressing again is the way out** (amended 2026-10-13). A speed, a pause and a frame step each cancel a search on its way, and from the second sim update on are not acted on either, so none goes out bare. The pause waits here, where into a jump it is not sent, because a key's search does not pause a playing replay: the pause is still owed when the search lands. On a replay that reads paused there is nothing to send, and the press only empties the slot. The rules, the same for a speed, a pause, a frame step and another search:
  1. **The first press waits for the landing, however long.** It goes out when the search has landed in the gate's one sense of the word, which is 50 ms after the first reading that shows it: sent from 30 ms after a landing on, each of the three was acted on 6 of 6. This is "hold until it lands".
  2. **A press that arrives while another waits takes over at once.** Pressing again is how a user says "now". It is the slot's newest-wins rule, with a takeover where a silent swap would leave the newer press waiting too. A further detent of a dial turn and a held key's auto-repeat are not a second press: they replace what waits and wait too, so a quick flick of Frame Jog or Speed Control cannot cancel a long search; a dial press or a key acts at once (maintainer, 2026-10-14, choosing this over a second detent taking over, at the cost of a turn that does nothing after a search that found nothing on a paused replay until it could no longer be running). The gate's own follow-on in the slot (the playback step, a speed a takeover gives back) is not a press either.
  3. **A press left waiting alone takes over when the search is no longer taken to be on its way**: at the end of the time it could be running on a paused replay, and 400 ms after the send on a playing replay whose frame kept advancing. A press that arrives later than that, with the record still standing, takes over at once.
- **The takeover** (added 2026-10-13) is the route measured to be right whether or not a search was running: a pause if the replay is moving, an absolute jump to the frame beside the one telemetry reads, and then the press's command on that jump's landing. A jump into a search on its way wins and the search is gone; with no search it is a move of one frame. For a frame step the jump is the step, and it leaves a moving replay paused as Frame Jog's step does. For another search, the new search goes out once the jump's landing has held, and a replay the takeover paused then gets back the speed it had. The takeover clears the record of the search, so all of this happens to one press.
  - **Beside** is one frame forward: a frame that cannot be landed on resolves forward (the sim facts). It is one frame back only inside the end-of-tape margin, where forward cannot move.
  - **The takeover's jump is judged as a search's landing is, not as a target**: the frame moved and then held. Its job is to prove the jump was carried out, so a rest a few frames off at a session boundary still counts and the command goes out from there. If nothing moves within the jump's wait, the press is dropped.
  - **The speed it gives back is the one the replay had when the search went out**, never a reading taken while the search was on its way: a search clears slow motion (the sim facts), so a reading there says full speed. A replay jump that starts while the record stands captures that same speed, or Remember state would put back a speed the user never chose. A search pressed on a quiet replay in slow motion still ends at full speed, as today: that is iRacing's, and it is not put back here.
- **What that costs.** After a search with nothing to find on a paused replay, a single press waits out rule 3: up to about 21 s at the start of a six-hour recording, far less near the end of one, and about 84 s on a 24-hour one, unless it is pressed again. The maintainer chose that over a cap of three seconds (2026-10-13), which would have a press cancel any search that needs longer than that after it. A second press ends a search that was still running, and the search has to be pressed again; someone who wants a far search to finish presses once and waits. On a playing replay neither cost arises: the gate sees which it is.
- **A search waits for the read-back and then for the reading to hold**: three reads 50 ms apart, the read-back form of the 100 ms that worked every time where the first read lost the search. This is #801's rule, and its only home is here.
- **A command that waits takes the replay cursor at the press, as every command does today.** A press that sends nothing at all, Speed Increase already at 16x or Speed Decrease at its slowest, takes nothing, and a walk under way goes on (amended 2026-10-14, as built). A jump sequence under way sends nothing more, so a Play pressed during a fastest-lap walk ends the walk and does not wait for it. A speed or a pause pressed during a jump is the driver's own word and replaces the sequence's playback step, which is #1276's rule unchanged.
- **A replay jump that sends a position jump does not wait for a search** (added 2026-10-13). When it starts, the search is forgotten: its pause, if the replay is moving, and its seek go out as on a quiet replay, and the seek is what puts the replay right (the sim facts: 8 of 8 on a playing replay, and an absolute jump into a search 8 of 8 on a paused one). Next Incident and then a marker key inside 200 ms is the case. A replay jump that sends no position jump, which #801's search runner is, has nothing to put the replay right with: it waits for the search to land for as long as its own policy says, and otherwise settles the replay by the takeover's jump before its first command (_What it gives #1426 and #801_).
- **A sequence still in its pause always gets a seek** (added 2026-10-12, amended 2026-10-13). A sequence whose claim is taken sends nothing more today, which is right for what follows its landing and wrong for a seek it has not sent yet: a Play pressed 30 ms after Next Marker on a playing replay would lose the jump, and the same Play 100 ms later would not. So whatever replay command takes the cursor in that window, a seek goes out. When the taker is a position jump or a frame step turned into one, it is the taker's own: sent at once like any jump into a jump under way, with no second pause, and it settles what the sequence owed, so nothing more goes out when the pause reads back. That was the third capture: a jump sent up to 35 ms after its pause, before the pause had read back, landed on its frame and rested paused 57 of 57, at 1x, 4x and 16x and across 60000 frames. When the taker sends no position jump (a press the gate holds, a pause it does not send, or a replay jump that waits on the target, which #801's search runner is and the gate's own jump for a waiting search is), one seek goes out to the target that stands once the pause has read back, as the sequence does it today: to the sequence's own frame, or to a newer one if a later jump has moved the target meanwhile and not yet sent its own. The sequence's own wait for its pause is kept as built; the captures say it could seek at once too, and that is a change to #1276's sequence this fix does not need. The taker owns only the playback step. The caller's own step after the landing, Jump to Fastest Lap's camera switch, still runs when the seek went to the sequence's frame: it belongs to where the jump is going, and a camera switch was measured safe. A target whose seek is never sent is cleared at that exit by whoever holds the seek, which after a takeover in the pause window is the taker and not the sequence that sent the pause: cancelled before it ran, the pause refused or never showing, the seek itself refused, or the claim taken by a key that sends no replay command. The give-up clocks start at a send, and here there is none. A press waiting behind such a target is dropped with it (amended 2026-10-14, as built): the jump it waited for did not happen.
- **A car or camera key is outside the gate.** It sends no replay command, a camera change harms neither a jump (the fourth capture: 9 of 9 on their frames) nor a search on its way (8 of 8), and what it does to a jump is as built: it takes the cursor to stop a fastest-lap walk searching for another car, a sequence it cancels in its pause sends no seek, and one it cancels during its seek lands with no playback step, which is #1428 and is not changed here. The jump's own camera switch is not sent after it: the driver's camera choice is the newer one.
- **A camera switch a replay jump makes for itself is sent only when nothing is under way** (added 2026-10-13). iRacing drops a camera change that arrives during a seek: the broadcast to another car 100 ms into a jump left the camera where it was 3 of 3, and the Next Car key 9 of 9. The sequence's own switch, after its landing, is so as built. One sent before the first command (the fastest-lap walk's, and Jump to Race Start's borrow of the pole car, #1354) and one sent when a walk gives up (#1354's restore) first wait until no jump is under way (the settled wait in its jumps-only form, since a search does not swallow a camera change); a wait that runs out sends the switch anyway and logs it at debug. As built (amended 2026-10-14): the fastest-lap walk's switch runs inside the walk, once it holds the cursor and its in-flight slot, not at the press before it, since a wait ahead of the claim would let a second press through and let a Play pressed meanwhile be overridden by the walk that started after it; and each wait holds the press's claim, so a key that takes the cursor while one waits ends the press and no switch follows it, the cancelling command's effect standing as everywhere else. When Jump to Race Start's last jump has stopped short, its target still stands while the press holds the cursor, so the camera goes back only once the wait runs out, up to about three seconds, with a debug line saying why (maintainer, 2026-10-14: kept as built). All three sites change with this fix, since #1354 is on `master` before it starts. The two press-time switches are a shipped fault of Jump to Fastest Lap by search: pressed during a marker jump or a dial spin, the switch is swallowed and the search follows the car on screen. What the search then does was traced by reading #1354's branch, not measured: its bisection reads the target car's own lap and lands near the right lap, its lap steps and its nudge are iRacing's lap searches and stop where the car on screen crosses a line, and its check of the target car on that frame fails, so it reports that the walk did not converge and records nothing. Jump to Fastest Lap rests on that frame with the warning; Jump to Race Start takes the frame only when the target car is within its bound of its own line there, and otherwise gives up and puts the replay back. No wrong lap reaches the record. A test pins it when this is built.
- **A search that waits behind a jump sequence carries that jump's playback on.** Otherwise the same key pressed slowly would leave the replay playing and pressed quickly would leave it paused, and #1426's commonest miss is exactly that: two record jumps toward the end of what was recorded, then a press the record cannot answer. So the waiting search inherits the speed the sequence captured, as a jump that takes over from a jump does today, and when the search has landed the gate applies the **Replay jump playback** step. Search first and playback after: a search on the held reading of a paused landing is the measured case. A search that found nothing is no longer taken to be on its way after the time it could have run, and the step then goes out by the takeover's jump, never bare. A jump that ends off its target sends nothing more, as now. A search pressed on a quiet replay is unchanged: no pause and no playback step. Behind a dial's frame move there is no captured speed and nothing to carry.
- **A claim holder's own commands go through the gate too.** The jump sequence's pause is not sent while a target stands (today's takeover rule, now also true when the jump under way is a dial's), its seek is a jump, and its playback step goes out after its own read-back wait.

## The waiting slot

One command waits at a time, for the whole plugin, and the newest wins: a second Play replaces the first, a search replaces a waiting speed. A pause empties it, and behind a search on a moving replay it then waits in it itself. Behind a search a newer press does more than replace the one that waits: it takes over (above). So does a new jump, which is a newer word about where the replay should be: a waiting search was meant to start from the old target, and a frame move is a request to stand still. That holds for every replay jump (`beginReplayJump`), whatever it sends first, #801's search runner included, whose first send is a pause or a search and not a position jump. A jump that starts while a speed waits takes that speed as the one it captured, so the **Replay jump playback** setting decides what becomes of it, and the speed is not sent on the read-back the jump is itself waiting for.

The slot is checked on a 20 ms timer (`REPLAY_JUMP_POLL_MS`) that runs only while something waits. It decides one thing: send, take over or drop. A dropped command writes a debug line saying what was dropped and why, and the state seam reports it.

**How long a press waits** (amended 2026-10-13; the first version dropped every press after three seconds). Behind a position jump: for as long as the target stands, which is the jump's own wait, and it is dropped only when the jump ends off its target or that wait runs out. A press made early in a ten-second jump across a six-hour recording means "when you get there", and that is not a press that fires late. Behind a search: until the landing, a second press, or the end of the time the search could be running. No press is dropped behind a search; each of the three ways out acts.

## What it gives #1426 and #801

Two waits, exported from the gate, each taking its time bound from the caller and resolving settled, gave up, or superseded by another replay command:

- **Wait until settled, holding N reads.** Nothing under way, shown on N reads 50 ms apart. It takes the caller's claim or none (added 2026-10-13). With a claim it ends superseded when the claim is taken. Without one it ends settled or gave up only, and a jump that starts meanwhile is simply still under way, so the caller goes on waiting inside its own bound. A second form counts jumps only (a target stands or a seek shows) and not a search that has not landed. It is for a camera tap, which a seek swallows and a search does not: #801's Car mode, and #1435.
- **Wait for a search to land.** The frame moved by more than playback explains, then two out-of-seek reads 50 ms apart that agree within what playback explains. A timeout means not landed; an unmoved search and a lost one look the same, and the caller decides whether to try again. The test is unchanged by the captures. On a playing replay the wait says more, which is what the gate's own slot already goes by (added 2026-10-13, for the first search of #801's run): **found nothing** when the frame is still advancing as playback explains one Fastest Lap Search Delay after the send, and **seen on its way** once the frame has stood still. From then the caller's patience no longer applies, and the wait goes on to the landing under a second, hard bound of the caller's. So it takes two numbers, a patience for a search nothing shows and a hard bound, and resolves landed, found nothing, not landed (the patience ran out with nothing seen, the paused replay's case), gave up (the hard bound) or superseded. What they add: a speed, a pause or a frame step sent after a timeout must not go out bare, since a search still on its way would be cancelled and the frame left standing.
- **Settle by a jump** (added 2026-10-13): the takeover's own route, for a caller whose search did not land within its patience. A pause if the replay is moving, then an absolute jump to the frame beside the reading, judged as moved and then held. After it a retry or a playback step is safe, whatever the search was doing. It says so when nothing moved within the jump's wait, and the caller then sends nothing more.
- **How long a search could still be running** (the distance to the tape's edge in its direction, doubled, never under the fastest-lap walk's wait) is exported as a number, for a caller that wants to wait that long. Nothing obliges one to.

Policy stays with the caller. #801's runner keeps its signed count, its clamp of ten, its one retry and its playback step, built on the two waits and the settle step, with a patience of its own: at the end of a list it should cost one or two of those, not twice the time a search could run across the recording. What it gives up is a search further away than its patience covers. #1426's Lap and Session keys step from the shared target while it stands and from the debounced frame otherwise, and its miss path sends a search through the gate like any other key. Both specs are amended to say so.

## The state seam

The gate's state can be read (added 2026-10-13; the coordinator put it in this issue so that the state machine's owner exposes it once and #1437 never edits the gate). It adds no behaviour: the gate sends the same commands in the same order with and without a reader, and a listener that throws is caught and logged and can neither hold nor drop a press. The names say what the gate knows, not what a key draws; they were agreed with #1437's author by message.

- **An origin token on each send**: an optional opaque string, never read by the gate. Whatever the gate sends on a press's behalf carries that press's token: a takeover's jump, a waiting search once it goes out, the playback step that follows it. `beginReplayJump` takes the token, and `end` takes a result from its caller (landed, gave up, cancelled). A key passes its context id. A dial passes its own on a push, a gesture and a Replay Markers detent, and none on a Frame Jog or Speed Control detent. A send with no token has no view.
- **A view per token**, with two facts that can both hold (a search key pressed twice has one search on its way and one waiting):

| Fact | Carries | Holds while |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `underWay` | `since`, `seen` | this token's target stands, its search may be on its way, or its replay jump holds the cursor |
| `waiting` | `since` | this token's command is in the slot |

- **`seen`** is whether telemetry shows it: true while a seek shows or a playing replay's frame has stood still, false for a search on a paused replay and for a jump still in its pause.
- **A fact is dated from the first command of its token's unbroken run**, and `since` does not move while the run goes on. A retarget by the same token (a spin's next detent, a second marker press from the same key) does not end a run, nor does a held command going out, nor a takeover's jump: each keeps the time of the press that began it. What ends a run is the fact ending, in any of the three classes below; a command from the same token after that begins a new one. A retarget by another token ends the first token's fact as superseded.
- **How a fact ends**, in three classes with the reason underneath:

| Class | Reasons |
| --- | --- |
| `completed` | `read-back` (a target), `landed` (a search), `jump-landed`, `sent-on-landing`, `took-over` |
| `superseded` | `retargeted`, `forgotten` (a jump or a takeover ended the search), `claim-taken`, `replaced`, `cleared-by-pause` |
| `gave-up` | `off-target`, `wait-ran-out`, `nothing-moved`, `walk-gave-up`, `refused`, `disconnected`, `replay-left` |

- **A search's fact ends** at its landing; when it is no longer taken to be on its way (`nothing-moved`); or when a jump or a takeover cancels it (`forgotten`). The record the gate keeps for its own caution can stand longer than the fact, and is not in the view.
- **When the sim goes away or the replay is left.** A disconnect ends every fact at once and empties the slot. The replay being left ends a search, a replay jump with no target and a waiting press when the debounced replay state says so. A standing target is the exception: a seek and the garage read the same, so its readings outside a replay are taken as its seek until its jump's wait runs out. That is the longest a fact outlives the replay being left, and it has to be so for a press held behind a ten-second jump.
- **One notice per change**, through `subscribe(listener)`, which returns its unsubscribe: the token, its new view, and whatever ended in that change. A held search that goes out is one notice, in which `waiting` ended as completed and `underWay` began. A getter returns the same view.
- **The raw record** (the target, the witness, the search, the jump holding the cursor, the slot, and when the gate last went from idle to busy) has a getter too, for tests and the telemetry snapshot. #1437 does not read it: one judgement of "landed", the gate's.

## The seam, and what it costs

About 73 send sites in seven files change from `getCommands().replay.x()` to the gate. The files touched:

- **New:** `shared/replay-command-gate.ts` and its test; the single-sender test. Every send site passes its origin token as it is converted, so the indicator of #1437 touches none of them. #801's stepping does not exist yet and passes its own.
- **Moved to `shared/`:** `replay-control/replay-position-state.ts` and its test, and `planFrameMove` out of `replay-control-frame-jog.ts`. `keepReplayTarget` goes: the dial's commands no longer need to protect the target from the dial's own cursor takes.
- **`shared/replay-cursor.ts`:** the pending landing (`recordReplayLanding`, `pendingReplayLanding`, `clearReplayLanding`) is replaced by the shared target; a cursor take no longer clears it. The claim and the pending jump speed are unchanged.
- **`shared/replay-jump-playback.ts`:** sends through the gate; `tookOver` reads "something is under way" from it; `beginReplayJump` takes the origin token and `end` a result. Claiming the cursor clears the pending jump speed, which is why `beginReplayJump` reads it first, and the speed a standing search record holds before either; the gate's jump for a waiting search has to read it before its own claim too. The sequence and the target must share one judgement of "landed": today the sequence takes the exact frame from a sample that is in a replay, and the target takes a conclusive read-back. Kept as two, the sequence can read its landing a tick before the target ends, and its own playback speed would then meet a target still standing and be held as a waiting speed, with `applyReplayJumpPlayback`'s result no longer true. So the sequence's landing wait is the gate's read-back.
- **`replay-control.ts`:** 42 sites in `executeMode`, the fastest-lap walk and the key repeat; and the three camera switches a jump makes outside its landing step, which gain the settled wait.
- **`replay-control-dial-surface.ts`:** `heldSpeed`, `isPausedForMove` and the follow timer's send move into the gate; the surface keeps its redraw.
- **`replay-markers-ops.ts`:** its jump records the shared target and its anchor reads it. The marker surfaces' one-second, 60-frame rule for when a landing still anchors is replaced by the target's read-back.
- **The hidden legacy actions** Replay Transport (8 sites), Replay Speed (3) and Replay Navigation (11): the same swap. They are still runnable from older profiles, so they are not left out.
- **Tests** for each of the above, which mock `getCommands` today and will mock or drive the gate.
- **Docs:** `.claude/rules/encoders-and-touchscreen.md`, `packages/iracing-actions/CLAUDE.md`, the Replay Control and Replay Markers website pages, and a Bug Fixes changelog fragment (a key thrown by a Replay Markers jump shipped before the dial did). On the Replay Control page the Frame Jog sentence "A turn made while another replay jump is still landing, such as a marker jump, is ignored; turn again once the picture has arrived" is rewritten: it is not what happens today, and after this fix the turn steps from the frame that jump is going to and leaves the replay paused there. The new sentence, approved by the maintainer on 2026-10-14: "A turn made while another replay jump is still landing, such as a marker jump, steps from the frame that jump is going to and leaves the replay paused there." The pages also say that a press made during a search waits for it, and that pressing again acts at once.

Two open issues become one call from the gate each, and neither is made here. #1429 (Replay Markers treats a long seek as the replay being left) wants the SDK's replay state told about the plugin's own seeks, and the gate knows every one of them; for its own targets the gate already takes such readings as the seek (_The state seam_). #1427 wants one pending speed for keys, dial and jumps, and the gate sends every speed, a held one included: until it records them, a marker jump begun within a poll or two of a Play going out reads speed 0 and ends paused under Remember state, as it does today for a plain Play key.

## What the user sees

- A key or dial input made while the replay is still jumping acts when the jump lands, instead of throwing the replay thousands of frames: about 0.4 s later for a near jump on the maintainer's machine, and some ten seconds later for one across a six-hour recording.
- A Frame Forward or Frame Backward press during a jump moves one frame from where the jump is going.
- A Frame Jog turn during a marker or fastest-lap jump lands one frame from where that jump was going and leaves the replay paused there, whenever in the jump it comes. Today it loses the jump or the jump's playback, by its timing.
- A Pause during a jump leaves the replay paused on the frame the jump was going to.
- A Play, a Pause or a frame step pressed after a Next Lap, Session or Incident press acts when that search has landed, however far it had to go. Today it cancels the search and can leave the replay reading as playing with the picture standing still.
- Pressing again acts at once, from where the replay stood, and ends a search that was still running.
- After a search that found nothing, a single press on a paused replay acts once the search could no longer be running; on a playing replay it acts within about half a second.
- Jump to Live and Go to start pressed during a jump go out at once and win.
- A Next Lap, Session or Incident press during a marker or fastest-lap jump moves on from where that jump lands, and the replay then plays or stays paused as the **Replay jump playback** setting says, the same as when the two are pressed slowly.
- If the jump does not land on its frame, the waiting press does nothing.

The website says it in the wording Frame Jog's section settled on: quick inputs ride on iRacing's own replay seeking, and take longer on a slower machine or in a long replay.

**On a six-hour recording**, at this tape's rate carried on:

| Case | What the user gets |
| --- | --- |
| Next Session three hours away (about 5 s), paused, Play pressed half a second later | it lands, then plays |
| The same, Play pressed twice | the search is cancelled at the second press and the replay plays from where it stood |
| The same, the replay playing | it lands, then the press acts; the gate saw the frame stand still throughout |
| A search with nothing to find, paused, one Play | it plays when the search could no longer be running: up to about 21 s from the start of the recording, far less near its end |
| The same, Play pressed again | it plays at once |
| The same, the replay playing | the press acts within about half a second |
| Play pressed one second into a ten-second position jump | held, and it plays on the landing |

## What the captures showed

Four captures were not taken when this was written: the sim was down, and the spec went first (maintainer, 2026-10-12). Each was modelled, with what a different result would change. They were taken on 2026-10-13 on the tape and machine named under the sim facts, by the scripted probe, every case from a quiet paused replay unless it says playing. One model held, two changed the table, and one let a wait go.

- **Play, pause and a frame step after a search.** Modelled: a speed and a pause do not harm a search on its way; a step does. **Wrong for the speed and the pause.** Sent 50 to 150 ms after a lap search of 475 frames, as the capture was worded, all three were harmless (18 of 18), but only because that search had landed inside 20 ms. Into a search still on its way all three cancel it: 12 of 12 into a session search of 22375 frames and 12 of 12 into a lap search of 6774. Sent 0.7 ms after the 475-frame search the search was lost 6 of 6, at 5 ms 4 of 6, at 10 ms 0 of 6. So the third column of the table now holds all three. The spec had said a pause there would become "not sent"; it waits instead, for the reason under the table.
- **Go to end sent into a jump under way.** Modelled as a search that waits. **It retargets the seek**: 10 of 10 on the end, and go to start 6 of 6 on frame 0. Both now go out at once.
- **A pause and then an absolute jump at once on a playing replay.** Modelled as unsafe until the pause has read back. **Safe**: 57 of 57. A taker's jump in a sequence's pause window now goes out at once.
- **The Next Car key sent into a jump.** Modelled as safe, never passing through the gate. **Safe for the jump**: 9 of 9 on their frames (the key 20, 100 and 200 ms into a 60000-frame jump, and 100 ms into the slow path of a near pair). The tap itself did nothing: the camera stayed on its car in all 9, where the same tap on a quiet replay moved it 2 of 2. A car key pressed during a jump is lost today and stays lost here (_Out of scope_).

**The control for the fix** (Testing, step 1) was run the same day on `master` at `d251b7020`, through the merged dial code: a Frame Jog spin of 16 frames, then 50 ms after its last jump the Replay Control key's command. All eight were thrown. Play, the pause and the frame step each ended 7597 frames past the spin's target, twice each, the Play playing from there; Next Lap ended on frame 102289 twice, where the same search alone found 102769.

**Three more sets were run the same day**, each for a question the captures raised. How long a position jump takes by its length, since every wait worked out from a distance leans on it: the time grows with the length to the end of this tape. Whether a camera change harms a search on its way, since that decides if a car key can stay outside the gate: it does not, and it is acted on. And whether a jump sequence can start behind a search that has not landed: it can. The numbers are under the sim facts.

**The search wait was redesigned the same day.** The amendment above it gave a press behind a search a landing wait worked out from the distance, after which the press was sent. The maintainer objected: "I think the 1.6 second strict limit is wrong. What about a 24 hour race?" Carried to that length, one search with nothing to find would have held and then dropped every press for more than a minute. A first answer, a short patience after which the press cancels the search, would have cancelled every search further than 55 minutes away, which at six hours is the ordinary one. Two more captures settled what stands now: the takeover is right into a search and after one (14 of 14), and a playing replay tells a search on its way from one that found nothing (22 of 22 and 14 of 14). The rule is under _What each command does_; the maintainer took the wait with no cap.

Still unmeasured, and covered by the read-backs and not by any number here: a slower machine; a jump or a search longer than 195000 frames, for which the longest recording the maintainer has is the capture that settles the rate and what telemetry reads through a seek of many seconds; the takeover on a playing replay as one chain (its parts are measured: the pause, a jump and Play, 8 of 8); Previous Lap and Previous Session sent from one, two and six frames past a line the cursor was put on, which #801's runner asks (the line before, or back on the same one); a camera change into a lap or an incident search; Jump to Live in a live session; go to start or go to end sent into a search on its way (modelled: it waits); a speed on the very first reading of a search's landing (the gate waits 50 ms more); and anything between 0 and 100 ms after a jump's landing is read.

## Out of scope

- **Commands from iRacing's own replay bar or keyboard.** The plugin cannot see them; the target's give-up and the witness are what limit the damage.
- **A car or camera key pressed during a jump** (#1435). The tap is swallowed while the seek runs (the fourth capture). The gate's settled wait is what a fix would use, and it is not made here. The dial's Car mode is #801's.
- **Making key searches add up.** With one waiting slot three quick Next Lap presses move two laps, never part-way. A count is #801's runner.
- **The record steps and the search runner themselves** (#1426, #801).
- **Telling the SDK's replay state about the plugin's seeks** (#1429).
- **The step-per-sim-update limit**: two frame steps inside one sim update still count as one.
- **A setting** for the waits. The Fastest Lap Search Delay is the one knob.
- **Knowing beforehand that a search will find nothing.** Next Session in the last session can be told from telemetry before it is sent, and would spare a paused replay its wait. Not done here.
- **Putting slow motion back after a search on a quiet replay.** iRacing clears it, as it does today.
- **What a key shows while its press waits** (#1437, built on the state seam).
- **The gate's record in the Telemetry Snapshot** (#1442). The record has its getter; writing it into a snapshot is a plugin-state section of its own, filed separately (maintainer, 2026-10-14).
- **Ulanzi dials**, which are not declared.

**Manual, in an iRacing replay**, on a Stream Deck+ and on the Mirabox knob:

1. Spin Frame Jog and press a Replay Control key in Play / Pause mode straight after: the replay lands where the spin was going and then plays.
2. The same with Frame Forward, with a held Frame Forward, with Next Lap and with Next Incident.
3. Press Next Marker and then Play / Pause on another key before the picture has moved; then Pause instead. Do both on a playing replay too, as fast as the two keys can be pressed, and with a Frame Jog detent in place of the key: the replay must always end at the marker, or one frame from it for the detent.
4. With the replay playing and **Replay jump playback** on Remember state, press Next Marker and then Next Incident at once: the replay must be playing after it lands, as it is when the two are pressed slowly. Again with the setting on Stopped.
5. Press Jump to Fastest Lap and turn a Speed Control dial during the walk: the walk stops and the speed is obeyed.
6. Press a marker key for the marker the replay stands on, then Play at once: it plays without a wait.
7. From early in a session, press Next Session and then Play / Pause as fast as the two keys can be pressed: the replay must land on the next session and play. Then Next Session in the last session and Play once: on a paused replay it plays after a wait, and pressed a second time it plays at once; on a playing replay a Pause there acts within about half a second.
10. On the longest recording at hand: a search that takes several seconds and a Play pressed once during it (it lands, then plays), then the same with Play pressed twice (it plays from where it stood); and a Play pressed early in a marker jump of several seconds (it plays on the landing).
8. Press Jump to Live during a marker jump: the replay ends at the end of the recording.
9. On a playing replay press Next Marker and turn Frame Jog one detent straight after, several times and as fast as the hand allows: the replay ends one frame from the marker, paused, every time.
7. An old profile's Replay Transport and Replay Navigation keys, pressed during a marker jump.
8. From the car: every key and dial does what it did before.
