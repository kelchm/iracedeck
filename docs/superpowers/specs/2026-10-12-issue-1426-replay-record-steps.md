# Replay record steps: lap and session jumps from the record, session starts and incidents recorded

> **Issue:** [#1426](https://github.com/niklam/iracedeck/issues/1426) · **Supersedes:** _none_ · **Superseded by:** _none_
>
> Point-in-time design record. The code and `.claude/rules/` are the truth; this is not documentation.

Split out of #801, whose dial modes are built on this. It builds on the per-session replay store (#1162), the lap record (#1203), the green record's recorder seam (#1354), the shared jump sequence (#1276) and the shared gate for replay commands sent while the replay is moving (#1430), and restates none of them. The order agreed on 2026-10-12 is #1276, the dial (#1316 and #1317), #1354, #1430, this issue, #801. The recording half here (session starts, incidents, the store) touches no key and may be built before #1430; the keys and the incident comparison go on top of its gate.

## The decision

1. **Next / Previous Lap and Next / Previous Session jump from the record.** The press finds its target among the frames iRaceDeck recorded and makes one waited jump there. A step the record cannot answer sends iRacing's search, as the key does today.
2. **Session starts are recorded the moment a new session is first seen live**, in the car or out of it, into a new `sessions` section. That is normally the tick the translator already detects the change on.
3. **Incidents are recorded and not yet used.** A new `incidents` section holds each incident at the frame it happened. Next / Previous Incident keeps sending iRacing's search, and a debug line compares where that search lands with what was recorded. Switching the keys over is a later issue, once the comparison has been read (maintainer, 2026-10-12).
4. **One walk rule for every recorded list.** Markers, lap starts and session starts are all a frame-ordered list stepped from the cursor, so they share the Replay Markers rule instead of each growing its own.

## The sim facts this rests on

Measured on 2026-10-12 over the 92 snapshots in the maintainer's `local/` that carry the replay fields:

- **In the car, `ReplayFrameNumEnd − SessionTime × 60` is constant for a session**, to within one frame: five sessions with two to four snapshots each (SubSessionID 86697546 reads 27994 at 73 s and at 133 s). So a session's start frame can be derived from any in-car tick. A negative result is a session that began before the driver joined, whose start is not on the tape.
- **Out of the car in a live session, `ReplayFrameNumEnd` counts the frames from the cursor to the end of the tape**, so `ReplayFrameNum + ReplayFrameNumEnd` is the newest frame recorded. Ten snapshots: at the live edge it reads 1 (five snapshots), and scrubbed back it reads the distance to it (1201 at 21 s back, twice; 27630, 34780 and 40182 further back). In each, `(SessionTime − ReplaySessionTime) × 60 − ReplayFrameNumEnd` is between 57 and 60. This settles the reading `replay-laps.ts` calls unconfirmed, and its comment is updated.
- **`SessionTime` stays live while an in-session replay is scrubbed back**; `ReplaySessionTime` follows the cursor (the same ten snapshots: 1704 s live against 1123 s on screen). Whether `SessionNum` stays live too is not measured, since every one of them is a single-session event. The design does not depend on it (see _Recording session starts_).
- **A frame recorded live sits about 59 frames before the same moment in replay numbering**: the figure above, and 59 exactly in the one session measured both in the car and across its saved file. This is #1162's `REPLAY_FILE_FRAME_LAG`, recorded and not applied.
- **Other cars' incident counts are hidden while an official session runs** (survey of 2026-09-21): `CurDriverIncidentCount` reads −1 for every opponent until the race is over. They were visible in the one hosted capture and are always visible with AI.

Measured the same day by a probe against the maintainer's sim, run for #1317 (a paused saved replay, around frame 15500; added 2026-10-12). His machine is a fast one and slower ones may seek more slowly, so every duration here is a lower bound:

- **A short position jump after a quiet replay lands in one sim update** (10 to 20 ms, a jump 12000 frames back included). **One sent less than about 500 ms after another takes a slow path**, and so does a long one by itself (60000 frames forward, twice, about 490 ms). On that path telemetry reads `ReplayFrameNum` 0, `ReplaySessionTime` 0, `IsReplayPlaying` false, `SessionNum` and `ReplaySessionNum` −1 and `ReplayFrameNumEnd` the whole tape's length (all 57 mid-seek rows of the trace) until the cursor lands. For two jumps that was 415 to 435 ms after the first was sent, whatever the gap between them.
- **During a slow seek an absolute jump retargets it and the last target wins** (10 of 10 at gaps of 20 and 60 ms). Fifty jumps 60 ms apart landed on the last one's frame, and telemetry read mid-seek for the whole three seconds. A jump relative to the cursor resolves from the start of the tape, and a frame step ends the seek wherever it has got to.
- **Out of the car at the live edge the raw `IsReplayPlaying` reads true** (the five edge snapshots above), so a tick that reads it false there is a seek under way.
- **Mid-seek telemetry reads `ReplayPlaySpeed` 0** (added 2026-10-12): 223 of 223 samples with the flag false, across the probe's thirteen traces. Every one of those seeks began on a paused replay, so what a seek begun on a playing one reads was not measured, and no jump of the plugin's begins one that way. What follows from it: a jump that starts while another is under way reads a paused replay and sends no pause of its own, and the speed to go back to comes from what the earlier jump captured (#1276's inherited speed), never from that tick.
- **A seek shows in telemetry only from about 20 ms after the jump is sent** (from the same probe, recorded in #1430's spec; added 2026-10-12). A reading taken sooner still shows the replay where it stood, so a reading that is out of a seek does not by itself say that nothing is under way.

And the searches, from the same probe's runs on that saved replay of 204563 frames: 30 cases, then 102, then the playing set again with its gaps corrected, then 47 for the captures this spec had named (added 2026-10-12):

- **A search after a quiet replay lands in 13 to 200 ms, with the old frame on screen until the new one.** No frame 0 on the way and no drop of the flag, except that a Previous Lap whose landing was frame 0 read one mid-seek sample first. An incident search moves the camera to the car involved, one sample after the frame.
- **Two searches on their way at once do not add up; one at a time does** (paused). Three Next Lap 200, 60 or 20 ms apart: the first landed at once and the cursor moved once more about 430 ms after the first was sent, to two laps on once and otherwise to a frame that is no lap start at all, in both runs. But a second search sent 100 ms after the first had read back landed exactly one lap further, 10 of 10 (Next Lap and Previous Lap, five each), carried out about 400 ms after the first landed; and two or three Next Lap 300 to 500 ms apart were right every time. Telemetry shows nothing in between that says a search is waiting.
- **On a replay playing at 1x** a second lap search about 115 ms after the first landed two laps on, 12 of 12 over two runs, and so did two Next Lap 300, 350, 400 and 450 ms apart, 4 of 4. Two sent inside one sim update counted as one, 4 of 4. Three in a burst were not tried there.
- **Incident searches did not repeat.** From one frame with the camera on one car, two Next Incident ended 0, 600 or 900 frames on from case to case, the pair sent 100 ms apart alternated between 0 and 600 from run to run, and a single one landed 300 frames behind the cursor: the search lands some seconds before the incident it finds. Putting the camera on one car before every search changed nothing: from one frame, five single Next Incident landed 300 frames back, on the frame, 300 and 600 frames on, and 300 back again. Each search stepped on from the last one's landing wherever the cursor had been put since, which reads as iRacing keeping an incident pointer of its own (an inference from the pattern). Searches sent one after another always stepped the same way.
- **A search sent in the poll that first reads a landing can be lost.** After a long jump's landing: lost 3 of 3, where one sent 100 or 300 ms later landed, 6 of 6. After a first search's landing: taken 3 of 3; after a second's: lost 2 of 3. A search sent 100 ms after the reading worked every time it was tried, 16 of 16; nothing between 0 and 100 ms was measured.
- **The opening #801's runner will send works:** on a replay playing at 1x, a pause, its read-back held 60 ms, then Next Lap, landed 6 of 6.
- **A camera switch does not end a position jump's seek, and does not happen during one** (amended 2026-10-13). Sent 100 ms into a position jump, long or on the slow path, the jump landed on its frame, 6 of 6 by the camera broadcast, and 9 of 9 by the Next Car key binding sent 20, 100 and 200 ms in (#1430's captures). But the camera stayed on its car in all 9, where the same tap on a quiet replay moved it 2 of 2, and of the broadcast's six the three that named another car left the camera where it was. Into a search it is different: the broadcast and the key sent 50 and 100 ms into a 22375-frame session search were acted on every time the change could show, 5 of 5, and the search landed 8 of 8.
- **A search sent during a position jump ends the jump where it stands**: on the start frame, half-way, and five frames short twice, in the four cases. An absolute jump sent 20 or 200 ms after a search won, 8 of 8 over the two runs. A pause sent 170 ms into a jump, with nothing after it, left the replay about 8700 frames off, 3 of 3: every replay command but a position jump ends a seek where it stands.
- **A search with nothing to find does nothing and shows nothing** (Next Session in the last session), so only a timeout tells. Next Lap in a car's last lap goes to the tape's last frame once, and then does nothing.
- **A search is a seek that telemetry does not show, and it takes as long as its distance** (2026-10-13, #1430's captures, recorded in full in that spec): 475 frames inside about 20 ms, 6774 in 68 ms, 22375 in 195 ms, which is iRacing's 2048 frames a sim update. A pause, a Play or a frame step sent into one on its way cancelled it, 24 of 24, and was not acted on itself once the search's first sim update had passed. After a search with nothing to find, each was acted on as on a quiet replay, 16 of 16. And a pause followed at once by an absolute jump on a playing replay landed on the jump's frame and rested paused, 57 of 57, at 1x, 4x and 16x and across 60000 frames. The same pause and jump sent 50 and 100 ms into that session search on a replay playing at 1x: the pause read back and the jump landed on its frame, 8 of 8, four with the pause read back before the seek and four with the two at once.
- **Not measured:** anything between 0 and 100 ms after a landing is read, and where an incident search lands after the cursor has been moved a long way with no search in between. They are step 8 under _Testing_.

## Stepping through a recorded list

A list is frame-ordered **recorded frames** (a marker, a lap start, a session start); the landing, the frame a jump to that entry sends, is worked out from the entry the walk picks (amended 2026-10-17). The walk is Replay Markers' (`replay-markers-ops.ts`), moved to `iracing-actions/src/shared/` so Replay Markers and Replay Control call the same functions:

- **The anchor** is where the replay is headed while a jump of the plugin's is under way, otherwise the cursor (`resolveAnchorFrame`). A second press during a jump therefore steps from where the first is going. Two things keep a seek's own telemetry out of the anchor, since a tick read mid-seek shows frame 0 and a walk from there would send the tape's first entry (amended 2026-10-12, twice). Where the replay is headed is one record for the whole plugin, which #1430 builds from the rule Frame Jog already has (`replay-position-state.ts`, #1317): every position jump registers its frame, and the newest one stands until that frame has read back in a way that can only mean the move is done. Failing that it stands for as long as the jump that sent it holds the cursor, however long the seek takes, which is the bound of #1276's own wait and the one these keys' jumps get; a target with no such jump behind it is given up a fixed time after it was sent. It takes the place of the cursor's pending landing and of the marker rule that ended one (pressed less than a second ago, the cursor not yet within 60 frames of it), which was a guess about the machine. And the cursor is the controller's debounced frame (`getReplayState().frame`, #1324), which keeps the last frame a replay tick showed while the raw flag reads false, for the second its grace lasts. A press that has neither, no standing target and a tick still reading mid-seek once the grace is over, has no cursor to step from; it is a miss and sends the search, which the gate holds while a seek shows (amended 2026-10-17: it sent nothing, where the keys had always sent the search).
- **Next** is the first landing more than 60 frames past the anchor. **Previous** goes back to the entry the anchor is in when the anchor is more than 120 frames past it, and to the one before otherwise. These are the marker windows, unchanged.
- **`steps`** is how many entries to move, at least 1. The keys pass 1; #801's dial passes the detents of a turn. The walk stops at the end of the list and reports how many steps it took. Zero steps is a miss.
- **The windows are measured on the recorded frames, and the landing offset is added after** (amended 2026-10-17, from the review). Measured on the landings instead, a cursor less than 60 frames into a session picked its own session's landing as Next: Jump to Beginning and then Next Session went two seconds into the first session.
- **A gap is not stepped across** (amended 2026-10-17, from the review). Laps are recorded only from the car, so a stint in the garage leaves a stretch with no lap starts, and a walk over the list would have stepped from lap 5 to lap 15 as a hit. A list may come with a rule for which neighbours are adjacent: then the anchor must lie in a recorded span (the entry it is in has an adjacent successor, or is the last entry of a list whose last span is open), and a step stops at the first pair that is not adjacent. Nothing taken that way is a miss, with the reason `outside the record` or `gap in the record`, and the key sends the search. Laps are adjacent within one session pair with consecutive lap numbers, and the last recorded lap's span is not open, since the laps after it may be unrecorded. Sessions are adjacent when the number goes up by one, or back to 0 under a new unique id (a restarted offline event), and the last session's span is open. Markers have no such rule and walk as before.

**Lap.** The list is every recorded lap start of the car the camera is on (`CamCarIdx`, verified by `carNumberRaw` like every lap lookup), across all sessions of the file. A step never crosses from one session's last lap into the next session from the record (amended 2026-10-17): the record cannot vouch that no lap came between, so that step is the search's. A landing is the lap start minus `LAP_START_APPROACH_FRAMES` (60), the lead-in Jump to Fastest Lap already uses. The store gains one reader, `laps.listLapStarts({ carIdx, carNumberRaw })`, and no format change; starts within 60 frames of each other are one entry, so a lap that two recorded sessions both hold is one step. Each entry carries its lap number and session pair, for the adjacency rule above.

**Session.** The list is the `sessions` section. A landing is the recorded start plus `SESSION_START_LANDING_FRAMES` (120): with the 59-frame lag, the raw frame lands in the last second of the session before, where the cursor would still read the old session and a second press would find the same start again.

**A miss sends iRacing's search**, the call the key makes today, and logs `record MISS (<reason>)` at debug. The reasons are the store's (`no file`, `no car`, `car mismatch`, `newer format`) plus `nothing that way`, `outside the record`, `gap in the record` and `no cursor` (amended 2026-10-17: a saved replay whose tick reads mid-seek past the grace, with no target of the plugin's standing, used to send nothing; it now sends the search, which the gate holds while a seek shows). The last one matters: the record ends where the driver left the car, and a step beyond it must still move the replay. The search goes out through #1430's gate, as every key's search does (amended 2026-10-12): at once on a quiet replay, and never into a jump or an earlier search of the plugin's that is still under way, since a search sent then ends the jump where it stands or does not add up (see the sim facts). Such a press is held until what it waits on has landed and is then sent (maintainer, 2026-10-12; `2026-10-12-issue-1430-replay-command-gate.md`), however long that takes (amended 2026-10-13: it was dropped after three seconds). One press waits at a time. Behind a position jump a newer press takes its place, and the press is dropped only when the jump ends off its target or the jump's own wait runs out. Behind a search a newer press takes over at once, by a position jump to the frame beside the reading and then its own command, and a press left alone does the same once the search can no longer be running. A miss pressed inside a record jump carries that jump's playback on: the search goes out on the paused landing and the **Replay jump playback** step follows once it has landed, or by the gate's takeover jump once the search can no longer be running when it found nothing, and never bare, so quick presses leave the replay as slow ones do. A Play / Pause or a frame step pressed straight after a miss's search is held by the same gate until the search has landed. Pressed a second time it takes over at once, and left alone it takes over when the search can no longer be running, which after a search that found nothing on a paused replay is up to about 21 s at the start of a six-hour recording; on a playing replay the gate sees within 400 ms that a search found nothing (amended 2026-10-13, twice: each of them cancels a search that is still on its way, and none is sent bare while the gate's record of the search stands). Quick presses on a miss can still move fewer steps than were pressed; counting them is #801's search runner, which the keys do not use.

## The jump

`beginReplayJump("replay-control-<mode>", telemetry)` at the press, its frame recorded as the target as the jump starts, before its pause (#1430), `runReplayJump`, and `jump.end()` in a `finally`. That gives the press everything #1276 decided: the replay is paused for the seek, nothing follows it until `ReplayFrameNum` reads the frame, and playback afterwards follows the **Replay jump playback** setting. Claiming the cursor cancels a Jump to Fastest Lap walk in flight, which is what these modes do today through `cancelFastestLapWalk`. The outcome updates the action's local speed, so the Play / Pause icons follow. The frame it records as it starts is the anchor of the next press (the plugin's one record of where the replay is headed, once #1430 has made the cursor's pending landing part of it), and a jump that ends any other way than landed drops it, since the replay is no longer headed there. On a playing replay the first jump pauses it and waits for the pause to show before it seeks. A second press in that wait is a jump into a jump under way: it moves the target and its seek goes out at once, with no second pause, and nothing more is sent when the pause reads back (#1430, amended 2026-10-13). Until the captures of that day the second press's seek was held until the pause had shown, because a pause followed at once by a jump had been measured from a playing replay only for a three-frame jump at 1x; it has now landed 57 of 57 (the sim facts). A record jump pressed while a miss's search has not landed goes ahead at once as well, on a playing replay with its pause first, and the search is forgotten as the jump starts (#1430; 8 of 8 in the sim facts): a jump that sends a position jump never waits for a search. The speed such a jump captures is the one the replay had when the search went out and not a reading taken behind it, since a search clears slow motion (#1430, 4 of 4); that is `beginReplayJump`'s to do.

**Nothing here waits on a clock** (maintainer, 2026-10-12). Each step goes out on what telemetry reads back: the seek once the pause shows, the playback step once `ReplayFrameNum` reads the frame that was sent. A timeout is a give-up that sends nothing more and leaves the replay paused where it stopped. Seeking is the sim's own work and was measured on one fast machine, so the Replay Control page says in user terms that quick presses ride on iRacing's replay seeking and take longer on a slower machine or in a long replay, in the wording the Frame Jog section settles on (#1317).

No camera switch: the lap list is the viewed car's, and a session belongs to everyone. Outside a replay nothing is sent (amended 2026-10-17: "as now" was wrong, since the keys sent the search from the car too, which iRacing ignores, and the gate checks only the connection; such a press now takes no cursor either). A target that still stands counts as a replay: in a live session a seek longer than the one-second grace reads as the replay left, and a press then steps from the target. Replay Markers keeps its own in-replay check first, so the two differ in that window. The four modes stay `api` in the comms catalog.

**Each press carries its key's origin token** (added 2026-10-13, for #1437's busy look, `2026-10-13-issue-1437-replay-key-busy-look.md`). #1430's seam takes an opaque token on every send, for a key its own context id, and #1430 passes it at the send sites `master` has when it is built. The record jump is not among them, so this issue passes the pressed key's token to its `beginReplayJump`, and the jump reports its result when it ends as every replay jump does (#1430). A miss's search is the call the key makes today, so it has its token from #1430 already; moving it behind the record lookup must not lose it. The incident observer takes no token: it sends nothing, the search it watches carries the key's, and a second fact for the one press would end twice. Nothing is drawn here. The looks are #1437's, which is built after this issue and #801 and touches no send site.

## Recording session starts

The translator already detects a session change on the tick it happens, and the Race Engineer's brief hangs on that. The record is written at the same moment, by a rule that does not need the edge:

**A session's start is `live frame − round(SessionTime × 60)`, clamped at 0, on the first tick a new `(sessionNum, sessionUniqueId)` is seen live.** On the change tick `SessionTime` is near zero and the start is the live frame. Seen later (the plugin started mid-session, the driver was scrubbed back in a replay when the session rolled over), the same sum gives the same frame, which is what the in-car measurement above shows. So there is one rule and one kind of entry, and no separate path for a change that was missed. A start that would be negative is a session that began before the tape did, and is the tape's start.

**Which ticks are live.** `replay-record-gate.ts` gains a second resolver beside `resolveReplayRecordTick`, which is left exactly as #1354 wrote it and is still the only gate the lap and green recorders use. The new one returns the same shape for two cases:

- **In the car:** `resolveReplayRecordTick`'s own answer, with the frame `ReplayFrameNumEnd`.
- **Out of the car at the live edge of a live session:** the controller's debounced replay state says a replay, the tick's own `IsReplayPlaying` reads true, the session is not replay-only (#604's `SimMode`), and `ReplayFrameNumEnd` is at most `LIVE_EDGE_MAX_FRAMES` (120). The frame is `ReplayFrameNum + ReplayFrameNumEnd`. The raw flag is the seek guard (added 2026-10-12): a tick read during a slow seek shows `ReplayFrameNum` 0 and `SessionNum` −1 with the flag false. On the measured tape `ReplayFrameNumEnd` read the tape's whole length on such a tick, which the edge limit refuses anyway, but that is one tape. The debounced state only picks the case; nothing is read from a tick the flag does not vouch for.

The live-edge condition is what makes a scrub harmless. If `SessionNum` turns out to follow the cursor in an in-session replay, scrubbing back across a session boundary would otherwise look like a new session seen from the garage. Scrubbed back, the cursor is not at the edge and the tick is not live, so nothing is read from it; the session is recorded on the first tick the driver is back at the edge or in the car, at the same frame. The module comment says there are two classes of tick and which recorder uses which: this one needs only the session's identity, its clock and a frame, and no per-car array.

**Two ticks must agree.** The recorder, `diff/replay-sessions.ts`, runs before the replay guard beside the others. It publishes a session's start once two consecutive live ticks give the same frame to within two frames, so a tick on which the session number has moved and the clock has not cannot put a wrong start in the file. A clock that stays stale for two ticks or more would agree with itself, so a start for a new pair must also come more than two frames after the latest start already published for the event; one that does not is dropped, and a later tick with a fresh clock records the session (amended 2026-10-17, from the review). Agreement does not catch a seek, whose readings hold still for a quarter of a second or more; the raw-flag condition above is what keeps those ticks out. It logs the tick's frame and `SessionTime` at debug when it publishes.

```typescript
"replay.sessionStarted": SimEvent<
  "replay.sessionStarted",
  {
    /** `WeekendInfo.SubSessionID`; 0 for an offline session. */
    subSessionId: number;
    sessionNum: number;
    sessionUniqueId: number;
    /** The live replay frame the session began at; 0 when it began before the recording. */
    frame: number;
  }
>;
```

The `sessions` section holds one entry per pair, in frame order, and the first one recorded stays. A restarted offline event puts the same `sessionNum` on the tape again under a new unique id (maintainer, 2026-10-12): that is a new entry further along the tape, and since every step is by frame, nothing here looks a session up by its number.

## Recording incidents

Both sources sit in one recorder, `diff/replay-incidents.ts`, before the replay guard on #1354's in-car gate, unchanged.

- **The driver's own car** from `PlayerCarMyIncidentCount`: a rise over the previous eligible tick publishes `replay.incident` with that tick's frame. No burst logic is copied from the Race Engineer's incident diff. The store folds a rise within 600 frames of the same car's last entry into that entry (ten seconds, #1122's sequence gap), so one crash is one entry at its first frame.
- **Other cars** from the session YAML's `DriverInfo.Drivers[].CurDriverIncidentCount`, where the sim shows it: a rise from a non-negative baseline. A −1 is no baseline, and a step from −1 to a number (the reveal after an official race) re-seeds silently, because the frame it belongs to is long past. A decrease re-seeds too, and so does a change of the row's `UserID` or `CarNumberRaw` (amended 2026-10-17: a driver swap or a car taking a freed index can raise the count too). The YAML lags the incident by an unmeasured amount; the comparison below is what measures it.

```typescript
"replay.incident": SimEvent<
  "replay.incident",
  {
    subSessionId: number;
    sessionNum: number;
    sessionUniqueId: number;
    carIdx: number;
    carNumberRaw: number;
    /** The live replay frame on the tick the count rose, recorded raw. */
    frame: number;
    sessionTimeMs: number;
    /** How far the count rose on that tick. */
    delta: number;
    /** The driver's own counter, or another car's count in the session YAML. */
    source: "telemetry" | "sessionInfo";
  }
>;
```

**Stored at the moment, offset at the jump** (maintainer, 2026-10-12): the entry keeps the incident's own frame, and the later consumer subtracts five seconds (300 frames) as a constant that can change without touching a file. No setting.

**The comparison.** After a Next / Previous Incident press, an observer waits for the search to land, claiming nothing (amended 2026-10-17: the gate's landing wait ends `superseded` when any other command replaces the search, which is what the claim was for), and logs at debug the frame and camera car at the press, the frame it landed on, the camera car as the later of its two reads shows it (the search moves the camera one sample after the frame), the nearest recorded incident and the difference in frames, or that none is recorded. The difference is logged raw and with the recorded frame moved on by the 59-frame lag, and the nearest incident is picked by the moved frame (amended 2026-10-17: the record is in live numbering and the replay is not). It sends nothing, and any other replay command ends it. It watches only a search the press sent at once; a press the gate held or refused is logged as compared with nothing. Landed means the frame has jumped, by more between two reads than playback at the speed read explains, and then two reads 50 ms apart, both out of a seek (the raw `IsReplayPlaying` true and `SessionNum` not −1), show frames that differ by no more than playback explains. That is the fastest-lap walk's `search` rule widened from a paused replay to a playing one: paused, any change is a jump; playing, the frame leaves its value by itself at once, so leaving it proves nothing (the probe's own read-back test was met by playback this way). A search that moves the cursor less than playback would is not seen, and is logged as not landed. The incident search steps a pointer of its own and not from the cursor (see the sim facts), so the comparison is between where it landed and the recorded incident nearest that frame, never the one a walk from the press's cursor would have picked; the frame at the press is logged so that the difference between the two can be read as well. The walk already refuses a sample whose `SessionNum` is −1, which is what a mid-seek tick reads beside frame 0; the flag is named with it (2026-10-12) so that frame 0 holding still for a third of a second can never pass for a landing. The Fastest Lap Search Delay is a floor under that read-back, as in the walk, and never the wait itself. A search that has not landed by the figure the gate exports for how long it could still be running, the walk's timeout or twice the time the distance to the edge of the tape takes, whichever is longer (#1430; amended 2026-10-13 from the walk's timeout alone, which a search across more than about 196000 frames outlasts; the gate itself no longer waits by that figure, and the observer may, since it sends nothing), is logged as not landed and compared with nothing. The wait is one of the two primitives of #1430's gate (the other waits until the replay has settled), written once there and used here and by #801's search runner. This issue does not write a second one, and the rule above is the contract asked of it (agreed with #1430's author, 2026-10-12).

## The store

Two more sections, `sessions` and `incidents`, each with its own module of pure logic and its own `version: 1`, added to `replay-session-store.ts` the way #1354 added `greens`: parallel to the others, changing none of them. Both follow the same compatibility rules: the envelope version does not move, an older build carries the sections through untouched, a section with a higher version is carried through and neither read nor written, and unknown fields survive a write. The unreadable-file merge keeps the file's entry where both have one.

**`no file` keeps its meaning.** `findLapStart` and `findGreen` answer `no file` for a record that was not loaded and holds neither laps nor greens. A record holding only a session start still answers that to them: nothing of theirs was recorded.

Four sections each carrying a parallel set of fields and branches is where a shared shape starts to pay. It is not done here, because it would rewrite two shipped sections inside a feature change; it is a hygiene issue to file once this has merged.

## Out of scope

- **The dial** (#801): the stepping modes, the multi-detent turn and the paced search behind a miss. This issue only makes `steps` a parameter.
- **Jumping to a recorded incident**, and anything that reads the `incidents` section.
- **Other cars' incidents in official sessions.** The sim does not show them while the frame is known.
- **Recording laps or greens from outside the car.** The live-edge frame would allow it, and a spectator's lap record is empty without it, but those recorders read per-car arrays and flags whose behaviour out of the car is its own question (`replay-laps.ts`). A later issue.
- **A Next / Previous Green pair** (#1354's designed extension).
- **A setting** for any lead-in or landing offset.
- **The legacy Replay Navigation action** (#1334). It gains nothing.
- **Generalising the store's per-section code** (see _The store_).
